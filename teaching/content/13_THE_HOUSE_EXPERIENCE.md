# The House
## How the whole library becomes one experience no other Bible app can copy

This is the product, not another folder of features.
If Emergent only ships rooms as tabs, we built a textbook.
If Emergent ships **The House**, we built the thing people cannot get anywhere else.

The WWJD companion already talks.
This layer is the house that companion is no longer allowed to skip.

---

## The one sentence

You do not open a book.
You walk into its house, live a morning in its street, hold one thing from the dirt, hear two honest chairs, look at the oldest ink, and only then ask what Jesus does with it.

That sequence is the product.
Break the sequence and it becomes every other app.

---

## Why this cannot be copied by a reading plan

YouVersion gives you a streak and a verse.
Logos gives you a shelf and a footnote.
The Chosen gives you a feeling.
A Sunday school gives you a moral.
A seminary gives you a fight.

Nobody gives you, in twelve minutes, on a phone, without picking a tribe:

- the story told at a campfire
- the actual house and tax booth
- one object that came out of the ground
- the sentence “this does not prove the verse”
- two full-strength chairs
- the oldest scrap we have
- the books your tradition never handed you, and who set them down
- a thread to Jesus that does not flatten Jonah into a sticker

That combination is the Rosetta Stone.
The companion is the person who walks you home after.

## Locked product decisions (do not reopen lightly)

1. **The metaphor is a house, not a course.** Courses grade you. Houses let you stand in a doorway.
2. **Rooms keep their names.** Door, Campfire, Passport, World, Map, Genre, Walkthrough, People, Two Chairs, Ink, Hard Ground, Thread to Jesus. No synonyms in the UI.
3. **Path is the default.** First visit always Path. Switching to Trail / Summit does not leave the room.
4. **World before argument.** You may not open Two Chairs until you have stood in World for that book. (You can skip Map. You cannot skip the street.)
5. **The companion may not quote a book the user has never entered.** If they ask “what would Jesus do about revenge?” and they have never walked Jonah or Matthew 5, the companion walks them to a Door first — thirty seconds — then answers. This is how we kill proof-texting without scolding.
6. **Two chairs are furniture, not a warning label.** The disagreement is part of the house, not a problem the app is embarrassed by.
7. **Ink is a pane of glass, not a lecture.** Slide Hebrew / Greek / a public-domain English / “oldest witness.” Never a wall of collation.
8. **You leave with one carried thing.** A sentence, an object, a question. Not a quiz score.
9. **Canon is a house with wings, not a fight in the foyer.** Protestant hallway first if that is the user’s Bible. Catholic rooms marked. Ethiopian upper floor marked. “Left out” is a room you can walk, never a smear.
10. **Jesus is the last room, not the filter on every sentence.** If you put him first, Genesis becomes a riddle and Job becomes a setup. If you put him last, you earned the sight.

## The shape of a visit

A visit is twelve minutes the first time.
It can become an hour later. It must be complete at twelve.

```
THIRST
  → DOOR          20 seconds   one sentence + one image-idea + genre badge
  → CAMPFIRE      90 seconds   the whole book as a story you would tell walking home
  → WORLD         2 minutes    dawn-to-dark + pressure map + object on the tray
  → WALKTHROUGH   3 minutes    4–8 movements, Path length
  → TWO CHAIRS    90 seconds   both sides at full strength, user not recruited
  → INK           45 seconds   oldest witness + one variant that actually changes a picture
  → THREAD        90 seconds   how this book helps you see Jesus, specifically
  → CARRY         20 seconds   pick one: sentence / object / question
```

Passport, Map, Genre, People, Hard Ground are **side doors** off the hall.
They are never skipped as if they do not exist — they are not required to finish a first visit.

A child stops after Campfire + World + Carry.
A skeptic will go to Chairs and Ink before they trust the Thread.
A grandmother may live in Campfire and Thread and never touch Summit.
All three had a complete visit.

## The first screen is not a library

Do not greet a thirsty person with 66 spines.

Greet them with three ways in. Same house.

### 1. “I have a life question”
Hands them to the companion, which must pick a Door, not a verse grenade.
Examples the companion is allowed to use as first houses:
- betrayal → Joseph movement in Genesis, or David in 2 Samuel
- empire / fear of the state → Daniel, Mark, Revelation
- revenge → Jonah, Matthew 5
- grief → Lamentations, John 11, Psalms
- “I don’t even know where to start” → Mark (shortest gospel, working village, no decoration)

### 2. “I want to walk a book”
A shelf, but the shelf is **time as a road**, not alphabetical.
Primeval memory → ancestors → Egypt → village → two kingdoms → exile → return → Greek pressure → the lake → the letters → the vision.
Ethiopian upper floor is a staircase icon, not hidden, not shoved in the user’s face.

### 3. “Show me what I was never handed”
Canon wing. One honest sentence: *different churches kept different rooms in the house.*
Then 1 Enoch’s Watchers fragment, Tobit at Qumran, Psalm 151 in 11Q5.
This is how we become the Rosetta Stone instead of a Protestant reprint.

## How the rooms play as one piece of music

Think of the twelve rooms as instruments. The visit is the song. Features played at once are noise.

| Room | What it does in the song |
|---|---|
| Door | the first note |
| Campfire | the melody |
| Passport | the date on the letter, in two columns |
| World | the body — heat, bread, tax, fear |
| Map | the feet |
| Genre | the key signature (do not read this as a newspaper) |
| Walkthrough | the verses of the song |
| People | who is in the room |
| Two Chairs | harmony that does not resolve for you |
| Ink | the paper the song was written on |
| Hard Ground | the notes that hurt |
| Thread to Jesus | the last cadence, not the only cadence |

World is the instrument other apps do not have.
Ink + Chairs together are the instrument churches are afraid of.
Thread last is the instrument that keeps this a WWJD house and not a museum.

## A first visit, written as if you are in the phone
### Book: Mark. User: anyone who said “I don’t know where to start.”

**Door.**
Night on a lake. A grindstone already going in a courtyard.
Badge: Gospel.
One sentence: *A working village meets a man who will not slow down, and a cross that Rome meant as a warning.*

**Campfire.**
A man named Mark writes for people who have already seen crosses. Jesus comes out of the water, the sky tears, and then he walks so fast the word “immediately” starts to feel like breath. He takes fishermen off their nets. He touches the people you are not supposed to touch. His own town tries to explain him away. Three times on the road he says he will be killed. They do not want that sentence. In Jerusalem the city eats him. A Roman officer looks up at the body and says the thing the book has been daring you to say since line one. The first witnesses are women. The last page, in the oldest copies, stops before anyone is ready.

**World.**
Dawn-to-dark in Capernaum. Her grindstone. His boat. The booth that takes a cut. The pressure map from Tiberius down to this alley.
On the tray: a lead net-weight.
Feel: follow-me costs supper.
Does not prove: this weight came off Andrew’s net.

**Walkthrough (Path).**
Water and a tearing sky → a village that cannot keep him secret → a road that only goes one way → a week in the feast city → a stone and a silence the oldest ink does not fill.

**Two Chairs.**
Chair one: Mark is Peter’s interpreter, written while witnesses are alive.
Chair two: Mark is a later church arranging memory after the war with Rome.
Both chairs keep the same village and the same cross.
You are not asked to vote. You are asked if the book still does what it came to do either way.

**Ink.**
Oldest substantial copies: Vaticanus, Sinaiticus. The short ending at 16:8.
Slide: some later copies add appearances. The app tells you which, and that the longer ending is beloved and late.
Feel: the oldest Mark leaves you in front of an empty place with your own feet.

**Thread.**
Jesus in this house is not a mascot. He is the one who will not stay clean, will not stay in Galilee, and will not come down. WWJD after this visit cannot mean “be nice.” It means: move toward the people the village avoids, tell the truth about the end of the road, and do not use power the way the booth uses it.

**Carry.**
Three cards, pick one.
- Sentence: *The oldest ending does not close the door for you.*
- Object: the net-weight.
- Question: *What would I drop if he said my name at the boat?*

The companion now has permission to talk about Mark.
It did not have it twelve minutes ago.

## What the companion is allowed to do after a visit

It may:
- use the carried sentence
- put the user’s life next to the pressure map (debt, boss, booth, grief)
- walk them to a second house if the first was only the porch
- remember which chair they leaned toward without baptizing that chair

It may not:
- quote a book they have never entered as if it were a tool
- flatten Two Chairs into “the scholars are just unbelievers” or “the church is just naive”
- skip Hard Ground when the user is standing in a violent text
- sell a reading plan instead of another room

The companion is the person on the porch.
The House is why they are not still guessing.

## Family mode (this is how all ages share one screen)

Two avatars on the same Door.
Left: Path. Right: Trail.
The parent hears the grindstone and the booth.
The child hears “a town by a lake, a boat, a man who would not stay away from sick people.”
Summit is a third toggle the parent can open after the child is in bed.
No one is told they are on the baby version. The names are Path, Trail, Summit — walking words.

## The wing most apps refuse to build

A door in the hall labeled **The rooms some houses never received.**

Inside, quietly:
- who listed books, when, and why
- Athanasius, Hippo, Trent, the Reformers, the Tewahedo church
- 1 Enoch’s Watchers in the dirt at Qumran, and the Parables booklet that is not there
- Meqabyan is not Maccabees
- “left out” is never “so it is worthless” and never “so your pastor lied to you”

A thirsty person who only ever owned a 66-book Bible should feel enlarged, not mugged.
A Tewahedo user should feel recognized, not exhibited.

## What we will not add, even if it tests well

- Badges that turn Scripture into a streak.
- A hot-take feed.
- AI that generates a new midrash and calls it “what the text says.”
- A single “true date” printed without a second column.
- Horror close-ups in Path.
- A Jesus filter that makes every psalm a prediction and every law a costume.

Those things get downloads.
They would make this house like every other house.

## The measure of whether we built it

A user who came in thirsty should be able to say, without notes:

1. What kind of writing I was in.
2. What the morning smelled like.
3. One object that came out of the ground, and what it does not prove.
4. Where honest readers split.
5. What the oldest copy does to the last page.
6. One true thing about Jesus that this book, and not a slogan, just showed me.

If they can say those six, The House works.
If they only remember a vibe, we shipped a mood board.

## Build order for Emergent

Ship the song, not the orchestra.

1. Shell of The House + three ways in
2. Mark as the first complete visit (we already have gold rooms + day + object)
3. Jonah second (short, chairs are clean, object is empire)
4. Exodus third (World engine is strongest here)
5. Companion gated on visited Doors
6. Canon wing with Enoch / Jubilees / “who left what out”
7. Promote compact books to full houses, Psalms and John next

Do not wait for eighty-one finished houses.
A thirsty person needs one true house this week.
Mark is that house.
