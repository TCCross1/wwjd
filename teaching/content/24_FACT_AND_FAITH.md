# Where facts meet the line of faith
## The seam this app exists to teach

Facts: dirt, dates, copies, empires, boats, bricks, bones of cities.
Faith: this is the Word of God, this Jesus is Lord, this story is for you.

Most Bible apps pick one and fake the other.
Study apps pile facts until the page feels like a museum.
Devotional apps skip the dirt so faith feels like a mood.

We stand on the line.
Every visit should make a person *touch both* and not be forced to throw one away.

---

## The two rails, on almost every screen

Left rail, small, always honest: **What we can point at.**  
Right rail, quiet, never salesy: **What the page asks.**

Example, Mark, the net-weight:

- Point at: lead weights like this came off Galilee boats. Fishing was a trade. “Follow me” cost a living.
- The page asks: will you leave the boat?

Example, Exodus, brick:

- Point at: Egypt counted bricks. Missing straw was a real punishment in their own pictures.
- The page asks: cry out. Come out.

Example, the empty tomb copies:

- Point at: the oldest big copies stop while the women are still running. Later copies print more verses the church has loved.
- The page asks: He is not here. Go to Galilee.

The rails never say “therefore you must join this church.”
The rails never say “therefore the bones disprove the Book.”
They meet. The user stands there.

---

## How people interact with the seam (this is the special part)

Not a quiz. Not a debate vote. Four gestures only.

**1. Hold.**  
They press the object or the old page. A one-line fact rises. They lift the finger. A one-line ask remains.

**2. Slide.**  
Ink: slide from “what the dirt shows” to “what the words say.” Same verse. Two lights. No winner banner.

**3. Linger.**  
After the story pictures, the screen does not auto-advance. One breath. Then the ask appears in smaller type. If they skip it, they skip it. Faith is not a pop-up they have to dismiss.

**4. Carry.**  
They leave with one thing from the seam — a fact they can tell a friend, or a line they have to live. The shelf stores both kinds. That is how the app keeps teaching after they close it.

WWJD chat uses the same two rails.

> In this book we can point at a tax booth by the lake. The page asks what you do with power. What is on you?

If they only want facts, the chat will still give facts, then one ask, then hush.
If they only want a feeling, the chat will still put their hand on the street first.

## Teaching without crossing the line we already drew

We teach:

- the story in order
- what kind of writing this is
- who was sweating when it was first heard
- what the oldest copies do
- where Jesus stands at the end of this book

We do not teach:

- a denomination
- a calendar of the earth as if it were a verse
- saucers
- “real Christians believe X about bones”

When a fact runs out, we say it ran out.
That sentence *is* teaching. It trains people to trust a Book that does not pretend.

When the page asks something a fact cannot answer — forgive, follow, believe He got up — we say so.
That sentence is also teaching. It trains people not to wait for a lab to finish their life.

---

## Make it feel special, not school

- Picture first, always.
- One fact. One ask. Then they move.
- Sound under the pictures (millstone, harbor) so the street is in the body.
- Return visits change the hour of the picture, not the homework.
- “Something on my chest” still opens a book, then the two rails on that book.
- Share card is one rail or the other, never a fight.

A person should leave saying two sentences:

“I didn’t know the boat needed a weight.”
“I cannot stop hearing ‘follow me.’”

That is facts meeting faith.
That is the app.
