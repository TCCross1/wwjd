# W.W.J.D. — Bible Teaching Layer

The WWJD companion already exists in Emergent. This folder is only the Rosetta Stone teaching model.

**Working name:** W.W.J.D. teaching library  
**Expanded:** What Would Jesus Do  
**Promise:** Bring a real human situation to Jesus — and understand the Bible well enough to recognize His voice.

Tagline options (pick one later):
- “Ask as if He were in the room.”
- “Scripture, not slogans.”
- “The Bible, decoded. The question, answered.”
- “A Rosetta Stone for the Word. A companion for the walk.”

## The problem

Most people meet the Bible in fragments:
- A verse pulled out of a fight
- A childhood story they only half remember
- A translation they cannot explain
- A preacher they either trust blindly or distrust completely

Meanwhile life is loud: money, marriage, anger, fear, work, illness, kids, shame, plans.  
“What would Jesus do?” becomes a bumper sticker instead of a practiced way of seeing.

W.W.J.D. exists so a 13-year-old, a new believer, a skeptic, and a lifelong churchgoer can all stand in front of the same text, see what it actually is, hear the live debate around it, and still walk away with a Jesus-shaped next step.

## Two rooms, one house

### Room 1 — The Companion (WWJD)
The user pours out an issue, a thought, or a plan.  
The AI does **not** play God, therapist, or denominational referee.  
It answers as a careful student of the Gospels who has also read the rest of Scripture:

1. Restate the human situation without flattery or scolding.
2. Name the heart under the surface (fear, pride, grief, justice, hunger, control).
3. Show what Jesus **did** in similar rooms (narrative first).
4. Show what Jesus **taught**.
5. Show how the apostles applied it — and where the Church later disagreed.
6. Name the tensions honestly (mercy and truth, peace and confrontation).
7. Offer 1–3 concrete next steps the user can take this week.
8. End with a question and, if the user wants it, a short prayer.

### Room 2 — The Rosetta Stone (Bible Learning)
Every book of the Bible becomes a creative learning module:
- Simple enough for a first-time reader
- Deep enough that a pastor does not feel talked-down-to
- Honest about authorship debates, dating ranges, environment, and purpose
- Mapped in geography and in time
- Transparent about translation choices and rival interpretations
- Always ends by asking: *How does this book show us who Jesus is, and how He walks?*

## Who it is for

| Person | What they need |
|---|---|
| Curious teen | Story, map, “why this matters,” no shame |
| New believer | A path through the whole library, not random verses |
| Lifelong churchgoer | Fresh depth without novelty for novelty’s sake |
| Skeptic / wounded | Fair cases on both sides, no ambush |
| Parent / teacher | A module they can sit with a child |
| Pastor / student | Sources, debates, translation notes they can trust |

## What it is not

- Not a denomination in an app
- Not a replacement for a church, a pastor, or a friend
- Not a fortune-teller (“Jesus says take this job”)
- Not medical, legal, or crisis counseling
- Not a weapon for winning arguments
- Not “AI Jesus” speaking in first person as if it *is* Christ

The voice is a well-read, humble companion who keeps pointing at Jesus and the text.

## North-star principle

**Show the map. Show the forks in the road. Leave the walking to the user.**

When scholars disagree, the app says so.  
When churches disagree, the app says so.  
When the original language can be read two honest ways, the app says so.  
Then it still answers the living question: *given who Jesus actually was, what would faithfulness look like here?*
