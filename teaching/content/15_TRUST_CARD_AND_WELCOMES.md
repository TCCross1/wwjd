# Trust Card and tradition welcomes
## Ship these words. Do not rewrite colder.

First-run order:
1. Trust Card (cannot skip, can be revisited from Settings)
2. “Which house did you grow up in?”
3. The matching welcome (one screen)
4. First Door: Mark, unless the user came in on a life question

---

## Screen 1 — Trust Card

**Title:** How this house treats the Bible

We love this library.
We receive it as the church’s Holy Scripture — God-breathed, able to make you wise for salvation in Jesus Christ.

God used real people in real weather. That is not a threat to the Book. That is how He chose to speak.

Christians who love Jesus do not always agree on dates, authors, or which books sit on which shelf. We will not hide those family arguments. We will not mock either chair. We will not ask you to pick a team in the foyer.

This app is a teacher and a map.
It is not your church.
It is not your pastor.
It is not the Holy Spirit.

If a room ever feels like someone is trying to take the Book from you, that room has failed. Tell us.

**Button:** I understand — show me in.
**Small link:** What we will never do

---

## Screen 1b — What we will never do

- Sneer at your grandmother’s Bible.
- Call a beloved passage “fake.”
- Print one date as if the family argument is over.
- Generate verses the page does not contain.
- Hide a seam so you will like us more.
- Delete the cross so you will like us more.
- Recruit you to a culture-war side.
- Replace your church with a feed.

**Button:** Back to the house.

## Screen 3A — Protestant / evangelical welcome

You are in the common hallway most English Bibles use: sixty-six books.

Other churches keep more rooms in the Old Testament house. We will label those rooms plainly — “read as Scripture in other churches” — never “fake,” never “added in the dark.”

When copies of a verse disagree, we will show you the seam and tell you this: no Christian hope hangs on a single disputed line. The risen Jesus is not sitting on a footnote.

If a chair in this house is not your chair, it still belongs to someone who prays. You do not have to sit in it. You do have to let them keep it.

**Button:** Walk Mark with me.
**Secondary:** I have a question from my life.

---

## Screen 3B — Catholic welcome

You are in a house that already knows Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, and the Maccabees as Scripture. We will use the church’s word: deuterocanonical. We will not call them Apocrypha on your door.

The sixty-six-book shelf is how many of our neighbors were raised. We will explain that shelf without insulting yours, and yours without insulting theirs.

Trent named a dispute in a crisis. It did not invent these books. They were read in the church long before that room.

This tool is not the Magisterium. Take what you learn back to the Mass and to people who can answer for the church.

**Button:** Walk Mark with me.
**Secondary:** Show me the Old Testament as my church reads it.

---

## Screen 3C — Orthodox welcome

You are in a house that knows the Scriptures as they are read in the liturgy — including books many English Bibles never handed you.

We will not flatten your shelf into “Catholic plus extras.” Your fathers received what they received. We will name those books as holy in your life, not as a curiosity.

This tool is a map. The services are the home. If a sentence here ever sounds thinner than the hymn, trust the hymn and tell us.

**Button:** Walk Mark with me.
**Secondary:** Show me the wider Old Testament shelf.

---

## Screen 3D — Ethiopian Tewahedo welcome

You are in a house that has room for the library your church actually kept — including 1 Enoch, Jubilees, and the Meqabyan books.

Those rooms are not an exhibit. They are an upper floor of the same house. We will not confuse Meqabyan with the Greek Maccabees. We will not talk as if your fathers were careless.

Other churches live in smaller houses. We will say that with respect, not as a verdict on you.

**Button:** Walk Mark with me.
**Secondary:** Show me the Tewahedo shelf first.

---

## Screen 3E — I am just arriving

You do not need a team to come in.

We will start you in Mark: a short gospel from a working town by a lake. You will stand in a courtyard, hold one thing that came out of the ground, and meet Jesus before anyone asks you to pick a chair.

The longer shelves — Catholic, Orthodox, Ethiopian — are upstairs when you are ready. Nobody will quiz you at the door.

**Button:** Walk Mark with me.
**Secondary:** I have a question from my life.

---

## Screen 3F — I do not want to say

That is a full answer.

Default hallway: the sixty-six books most English readers know. Every other wing stays marked and unlocked.

We will not guess your church from your tap.

**Button:** Walk Mark with me.
**Secondary:** I have a question from my life.

## Companion gate (lock this in Emergent)

Before the companion quotes a book, check `visited_doors[]`.

If the book has not been entered:
- Open that book’s Door for thirty seconds (title, one sentence, genre badge).
- Then answer.
- Never fire a verse as a tool the user has not met.

Exception: a direct “what does John 3:16 say?” may show the verse and immediately offer the Door. Quoting is not the same as using.
