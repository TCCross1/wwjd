# BibleProject brief
## What to copy. What never to do. Then stop talking about them.

They are the closest cousin to The House: beautiful, narrative, widely shared, and now a cautionary tale in the exact churches we need to trust us with their kids.

This is not a review of Tim Mackie.
This is a fence.

---

## Copy these

- The Bible is one story, not a quote farm.
- Genre is taught before application.
- Pictures do more than paragraphs.
- A teenager and a pastor can sit in the same room.
- Wonder first. Footnotes second.
- They made people *want* to open the book.

That last one is the whole prize.

---

## Never do these

- Get vague at the cross. If the book itself speaks of sin, guilt, blood, wrath, or a risen body, the Thread may not go quiet to stay likable. Romans especially.
- Let “unified story that leads to Jesus” flatten the Hebrew books before they are allowed to be themselves. Thread is last, not a filter on every sentence.
- Hide a statement of how we treat Scripture. Their missing-feeling “what do you actually believe about the Book?” is half the backlash.
- Treat a distinctive scholarly reconstruction (divine council, a particular atonement model, a Genesis framework) as if it were simply “what the Bible says.”
- Build a brand so warm that Hard Ground feels like a betrayal when you finally tell the truth.
- Assume beauty will carry doctrine. Beauty carried their downloads. Doctrine is why pastors stopped hitting send.

---

## How we use the lesson

Trust Card first.
Tradition on the left of the Passport.
Jesus last and specific.
Cross present when the book puts it there.
No sneer at the chair we do not sit in.

Then we stop measuring ourselves against their videos.
Mark is the measure.
