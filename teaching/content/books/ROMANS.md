# Romans
**Section:** Pauline letters  
**Genre:** Congregation letter (a real letter that thinks like a sermon)  
**Default map:** Eastern Mediterranean (Rome, plus Paul’s world)

Romans is the gold-master for “how to read a letter” and for Two Chairs that stay kind.

---

## 1. The Door

**One sentence:** Paul writes to Christians in the capital of the world to say that God’s rescue is for Jew and Gentile together, by Jesus, and that this rescue should change a body, a household, and a church.

**Image-idea:** A rented room in a noisy city. A scribe, a tired apostle, a stack of names at the end of a scroll.

**Time ribbon:** Mid-first century. Jesus has risen. Churches exist. Israel’s story has not been erased.

**Path line:** “A long letter about how God makes people right and how they should live together in Rome.”

---

## 2. The Campfire

### Path
Paul has never been to the church in Rome, but he knows some of them. He wants to visit. He wants them to help him on the way to Spain. Before he comes, he writes the biggest letter we have from him.

He says the whole world — religious and not — is in trouble. God has not left the world there. Jesus died and rose. People are put right with God by trusting Him, the way Abraham trusted. This is not a new hobby God invented. It is how the promise always worked. Jews and non-Jews now belong in one family. That does not mean God lied to Israel. It means the mercy is running wider than anyone expected.

Then Paul gets practical. Offer your body. Do not copy the city’s pattern. Honor one another. Live at peace if you can. Welcome the weak and the strong instead of winning food fights about holy days. Pay what you owe. Love is the debt that never closes.

### Trail
Romans is not a textbook that fell out of the sky. It is a letter to house-churches in a city that had recently expelled Jews (the emperor Claudius) and then let them return. Gentile believers may have grown used to running the room. Jewish believers returned to a church that had learned to live without them. Paul’s gospel of one family is therefore not only doctrine. It is conflict repair.

The letter’s spine: God’s righteousness — His rightness and His way of making people right — unveiled in the good news. Chapters 1–4 diagnose and announce. 5–8 sing what life in the Spirit is. 9–11 refuse to throw away Israel. 12–15 put a body on the theology. 16 proves this was always about real names.

## 3. The Passport

**Traditional author:** Paul the apostle, with Tertius as scribe (16:22).  
**Why people hold this:** The letter says so. Style and theology match the undisputed Paul.

**Other serious proposals:** Almost no serious scholar doubts Paul wrote Romans. A few debate whether chapter 16 was first meant for Ephesus, or whether 16:25–27 is a later doxology. Those are edge questions.

**Traditional / academic date:** A rare calm overlap. Most land at AD 55–58, written from Corinth or nearby, before Paul takes the collection to Jerusalem.

**Setting:** Paul in Greece; hearers in Rome.  
**First audience:** Mixed Jewish and Gentile house-churches in Rome.  
**Language:** Greek.  
**Purpose in one line:** To preach the gospel to Rome in writing, unite a divided church, and clear the road for a visit and a mission farther west.

---

## 4. The World Around the Words

Rome was proud of law, peace, and power. “Good news” was a word emperors used for their own reign. Paul takes that word and hangs it on a crucified Jew.

House-churches met in shops and homes, not cathedrals. Women like Phoebe carry the letter (16:1–2). Slaves and city officials can sit in the same greeting list. Food sacrificed to idols, Sabbaths, and ritual days are not internet arguments. They are supper-table fractures.

Honor and shame run the city. Paul keeps offering a different honor: being in Christ.

## 5. The Map

Pins:
- Corinth / Cenchreae — likely writing point; Phoebe’s church
- Jerusalem — where Paul is about to go with a gift
- Rome — destination
- Spain — the hoped-for next field
- Israel / Zion — the story-world of chapters 9–11

### On the tray
**Find:** A first-century Roman insula threshold / brick stamp from the capital’s rented stacked housing.  
**Feel:** Jew and Gentile in this letter share a stair in a crowded block, not a cathedral aisle.  
**Does not prove:** Phoebe knocked on this door.  
**Badge:** type-object · excavated class

This map is about a network, not one hillside.

## 6. How to Read This Kind of Book

**This genre does:** Speak to a real room about a real gospel, then ask that room to live a certain way.  
**This genre does not:** Hand you a verse to fire at a stranger with no chapter around it.

Rules:
1. Read 1–8 without skipping 9–11. Read 12–16 as the point of 1–11, not as leftovers.
2. “Law” in Romans is not the same word every time. Sometimes it is Moses’ covenant, sometimes a principle, sometimes a power.
3. When Paul says “Jew and Greek,” he is talking about God’s story with Israel and the nations, not a modern insult.

**Common misuse:** Turning Romans into a four-step pamphlet and never arriving at chapter 12. Or using chapter 13 to baptize every government. Or using 1:18–32 as a weapon while ignoring 2:1 — “you have no excuse, whoever you are.”

## 7. The Walkthrough

1. **The news and the problem** — 1:1–3:20 — Gospel first. Then a world that trades the Maker for images, and a religious world that does the same with pride.
2. **How God makes people right** — 3:21–4 — Jesus, blood, faith, Abraham as family father of all who trust.
3. **The new human life** — 5–8 — Peace, Adam and Christ, baptism into death and life, war inside a person, Spirit, no condemnation, nothing able to separate.
4. **Has the word of God failed?** — 9–11 — Israel, mercy, a remnant, grafted branches, a mystery, a doxology.
5. **Bodies in the city** — 12–13 — Living sacrifice, gifts, love, enemies, governing authorities, waking up.
6. **The weak and the strong** — 14–15 — Welcome. Do not despise. Do not force. Christ the servant of Jews and the hope of nations.
7. **The names** — 16 — A deacon, a house, a mother, workers, relatives, a scribe. The gospel has an address list.

Carry line (paraphrase of 1:16): “I am not ashamed of this news. It is God’s power for rescue — for the Jew first and also for the Greek.”

## 8. The People

- **Paul** — apostle to the nations, still in love with his own people.
- **Phoebe** — the likely carrier; call her what the text calls her, a servant/deacon of Cenchreae.
- **Prisca and Aquila** — tentmakers, church planters, risk-takers.
- **Abraham** — not a mascot for individualism; a father of a people.
- **Adam and Christ** — two ways of being human.

## 9. Two Chairs

### Chair set 1 — “The righteousness of God”
**Question:** When Paul says *dikaiosyne theou*, is he talking mainly about a gift God *gives* (being counted right), or about God’s own covenant faithfulness in action?

**Chair A — Gift to the sinner.** Classic Reformation chair. God declares the ungodly righteous through faith in Christ. Strength: chapters 3–4 and 5:1 live here.

**Chair B — God’s own rightness on the move.** God keeps faith with Israel and the world; the cross is how He stays just and still justifies. Strength: 1:16–17 and chapters 9–11 live here.

**Shared ground:** No one climbs into God’s family by moral résumé. Jesus is the center. Faith is not a work that purchases God.  
**Not settled by:** Treating the other chair as if it had never read Romans.

### Chair set 2 — “Works of the law”
**Chair A:** Moral self-help. Trying to earn heaven.  
**Chair B (often called “new perspective”):** Boundary markers of Israel — circumcision, food, days — used to fence the family.  
**Shared ground:** Whatever “works of the law” covers, they are not the door into Christ. Chapter 2 still names actual sin, not only badges.

### Chair set 3 — Israel in chapters 9–11
Some sit with a church-replaces-Israel chair. Some sit with a God-is-not-done-with-Israel chair. Some sit with a “remnant now, fullness later” chair. Steel-man at least two. Shared ground: Paul would rather be cut off than see his people lost, and he ends in worship, not a chart.

### Chair set 4 — Romans 13 and government
Some hear a call to ordinary civic obedience. Some hear limited endorsement of an empire that can also be “beast” in Revelation. Shared ground: Paul is not handing Caesar the keys of the kingdom. Love remains the larger command.

## 10. The Ink

**“Justified” / “made righteous” / “put right”**  
Same Greek family (*dikaioō*). Older English church Bibles love “justified.” Some modern ones say “put right” so a child can enter. Neither word is a full photograph. Teach the courtroom flavor and the covenant-family flavor.

**“Propitiation” vs. “mercy seat” vs. “sacrifice of atonement” (3:25)**  
Greek *hilasterion*. Can lean toward “place where wrath is met” or “place of meeting / mercy seat.” Families of translations split.  
*Shared ground:* Jesus’ death is where God deals with sin without pretending it is small.

**Phoebe as *diakonos***  
Servant, minister, or deacon depending on the English family. Do not hide that the word is the same one Paul uses for ministry. Let the user see why churches land differently.

---

## 11. Hard Ground

- **1:18–32 and the uses of this passage in modern culture wars.** Teach what Paul is doing: a mirror for the whole human race that swings around in 2:1 and hits the religious reader in the mouth. Do not pretend the text has no sexual ethics. Do not pretend the text exists to score points online.
- **Election in chapter 9.** Potter and clay language scares people. Show the pastoral end of the argument (mercy for the nations, hope for Israel) before turning it into a system.
- **Submission in 13:1.** Written under Rome, not as a blank check for tyrants. Pair with the rest of Scripture where servants of God refuse idolatrous commands.

---

## 12. The Thread to Jesus

Paul’s Jesus is the risen Son descended from David (1:3–4). He is the faithful human where Adam failed, the mercy-seat, the end of the law-as-ladder, the reason Gentiles sing with Israel’s Scriptures. Romans is what it looks like when someone who once fought Jesus spends the rest of his life explaining Him.

**Following Him:** In Rome terms — sit at a table with people you did not choose. Offer your body. Let mercy, not winning, set the house rules.

**Remember**
1. Romans is a letter to a city, not a pile of independent slogans.
2. The gospel makes one family without erasing Israel’s story.
3. Chapters 12–16 are not optional.

Read Romans 12:1–21 as if it were the assignment for your actual street. Circle one verb and do it this week.
