# 1–3 Meqabyan and 4 Baruch
**Shelf:** Tewahedo  
**Badge:** Tewahedo distinct

These four are why a Western reader keeps getting the Ethiopian Bible wrong.

---

## 1–3 Meqabyan (Ethiopian Maccabees)

### The one fact that must sit on the Door
They are **not** Greek 1, 2, 3, or 4 Maccabees.
They share a name about people who would rather die than worship idols. The stories, heroes, and chapters are different.

**Who keeps them:** Ethiopian and Eritrean Tewahedo churches only, as Scripture.  
**Who set them aside:** everyone else — not by a dramatic vote to “ban Meqabyan,” but because these books lived in Ge’ez and were never on the table at Jamnia, Hippo, Carthage, or Trent.

**Oldest copies:** Ge’ez manuscript tradition. No Dead Sea Scroll. No Septuagint column.

**What they are (plain):**
- **1 Meqabyan** — a long narrative of pagan kings, idolatry, and families who refuse to bow; names and plot do not match Judas Maccabeus’ war.
- **2 Meqabyan** — a shorter work about a king and three brothers who stand against idolatry.
- **3 Meqabyan** — further exhortation and story in the same Ethiopian cycle.

**How to teach them:**
Campfire + purpose + “not the Greek Maccabees” banner.
Do not pretend we have a 2nd-century Hebrew first draft. We do not.

**Chair A:** A national church has the right to receive the stories that formed its martyrs.  
**Chair B:** Canonicity for the worldwide church followed older, wider-copied Jewish and apostolic books. A late regional cycle can be holy history without being everyone’s Old Testament.

**Shared ground:** Faithfulness unto death is a biblical theme whether you are reading 2 Maccabees 7 or 1 Meqabyan.

## 4 Baruch (Paralipomena of Jeremiah / Teref Baruch)

**Door:** After Jerusalem falls, Jeremiah, Baruch, and Abimelech the Ethiopian are written into a hope-story that the city and the people will not stay dead forever.

**Who keeps it:** Tewahedo OT (counted with the Jeremiah family).  
**Who set it aside:** Jews; Protestants; Catholics as dogma; most Eastern lists treat it as outside the line even when they know it.

**Date:** Usually placed in the first or second century AD, after the destruction of the Second Temple — a Jewish (and then Christian-used) sequel, not a 6th-century BC diary.

**Oldest witnesses:** Greek; also Ge’ez, and versions in other Christian languages. Not a Qumran biblical book.

**Why Ethiopia kept it:** It travels with Jeremiah. It honors an Ethiopian figure in the story. It preaches restoration after ruin.

**Thread:** Christians later heard resurrection and even gospel overtones in it. Label those as later hearings. The Jewish wound underneath is 70 AD, or the memory of 586 BC used again.
