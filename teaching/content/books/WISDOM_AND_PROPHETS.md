# Wisdom, five megillot extras already covered, and the Prophets
Compact modules. Expand to twelve rooms later.

---

## Job
**Door:** A good man’s disaster becomes a long argument about God, justice, and speeches that are too sure.  
**Passport:** Author unknown. Setting feels old (patriarchal). Dates proposed from 2nd millennium to 5th c. BC. Purpose: to break cheap religion without breaking faith.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 3rd–2nd c. BC; paleo-Hebrew Job; Aramaic Job at Qumran (4 copies). LXX Job is shorter and in some Greek ends with a resurrection note.  
**Chairs:** Historical Job vs. a wisdom drama. God’s speeches as answer vs. as the end of answering.  
**Jesus thread:** A righteous sufferer who will not curse God. Christians have long heard a rhyme; Job is not a secret biography of Jesus.

## Psalms
**Door:** Israel’s prayerbook — praise, rage, confession, hope — given back to God in song.  
**Passport:** Many authors (David, Asaph, sons of Korah, Moses in Ps 90, others). Grown from the monarchic age into the Second Temple.  
**Oldest copy:** Most-copied biblical book at Qumran (36–39). 11Q5 orders psalms differently and includes Psalm 151 + other songs. Ketef Hinnom is not a psalmbook but shows blessing language already in the 7th c. BC.  
**How to read:** Poetry. Parallel lines. Do not turn a groan into a proverb.  
**Jesus thread:** Jesus prays the Psalms (22 on the cross). The church has never had another first hymnal.

## Proverbs
**Door:** Wisdom for ordinary days. The fear of the LORD is the start of skill, not of panic.  
**Passport:** Solomon as heading and fountain; also Agur, Lemuel, later gatherers. Grown over centuries.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 1st c. BC (few copies).  
**Chair:** Solomon’s court collection vs. a long editorial river.  
**Jesus thread:** “Something greater than Solomon.” James will sound like this book in Greek dress.

## Ecclesiastes
**Door:** A blunt look at work, death, and pleasure under the sun — and the call to fear God anyway.  
**Passport:** “Qoheleth.” Tradition — Solomon. Many scholars — a later wisdom teacher using a royal voice.  
**Oldest copy:** 4QQoh, 2nd c. BC (2 copies) — early enough to restrain very late datings.  
**Chair:** Despair vs. sober joy. The epilogue (12:9–14) as the author’s point vs. a later fence.  
**Jesus thread:** The Son who talks about storing treasure and losing a soul is not allergic to Qoheleth’s honesty.

## Song of Songs
**Door:** Love poetry in the canon. Desire spoken without shame.  
**Passport:** Heading links Solomon. Date disputed.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 2nd c. BC–AD (4 copies; some look short).  
**Chairs:** Human marriage song vs. allegory of God and Israel / Christ and the church. Best teaching: start with the human poem. Let the allegory be a second hearing, labeled as such.  
**Jesus thread:** The New Testament never quotes it directly. The church still heard covenant love here. Say that.

## Isaiah
**Door:** From a throne that is holy-holy-holy to a suffering servant and a new creation.  
**Passport:** Isaiah of Jerusalem, 8th c. BC, is the named prophet. Chair A: one prophet (and his school) saw the whole. Chair B: chs. 40–55 speak to exile; 56–66 to the return — a book that grew.  
**Oldest copy:** **1QIsaᵃ ~125 BC**, almost all 66 chapters, plus ~20 other Isaiah scrolls. This is the showpiece of ancient comparison.  
**Ink:** 7:14 *almah* / *parthenos*; the servant songs; “Holy One of Israel” tying the book together.  
**Jesus thread:** Jesus reads Isaiah 61 in Nazareth. The church reads the servant as Him. That is a Christian hearing built on a Jewish book.

## Jeremiah
**Door:** A reluctant prophet walks his city into exile and still buys a field as a bet on God’s future.  
**Passport:** Jeremiah and Baruch. 7th–6th c. BC events.  
**Oldest copy:** 4QJerᵃ ~225–175 BC. **Two editions at Qumran:** longer (like MT) and shorter / differently ordered (like LXX). Jeremiah is the master class in “the Bible already had more than one ancient shape.”  
**Jesus thread:** The new covenant in Jeremiah 31 is quoted at the heart of the New Testament.

## Lamentations
**Door:** Five poems for a burned city. Faith that tells the truth to God about wreckage.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Jeremiah. Many scholars — temple singers after 586 BC.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS late 1st c. BC (4).  
**Jesus thread:** Holy Saturday reading. A faith that can sit in ash without lying.

## Ezekiel
**Door:** Visions in exile: a wheeled throne, a valley of bones, a temple that teaches hope.  
**Passport:** Ezekiel the priest-prophet, 6th c. BC.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 1st c. BC (6).  
**How to read:** Vision. Do not turn wheels and faces into a machine manual.  
**Jesus thread:** “Son of man” as address. Shepherd of Israel. Water from the temple. John and Revelation drink here.

## Daniel
**Door:** Faithful young exiles — and night visions of beasts and a human figure who receives a kingdom.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Daniel in the 6th c. BC. Many scholars — finished in the 160s BC under Antiochus IV, using older court tales. Both chairs must be written at full strength.  
**Oldest copy:** 8 DSS copies from late 2nd / 1st c. BC — close to the critical date of the finished book. Greek Daniel is longer (Susanna, Bel, Song of the Three): Catholic/Orthodox/Tewahedo keep the additions.  
**Jesus thread:** “Son of Man coming with the clouds” (Dan 7) is Jesus’ own title-language.

## The Twelve (one paragraph each)

**Hosea** — A marriage becomes a picture of God who will not quit. 8th c. BC north. DSS among the Twelve. Jesus: “I desire mercy, not sacrifice.”

**Joel** — Locusts, return, Spirit on all flesh. Date disputed. Acts 2 plants Joel in the street at Pentecost.

**Amos** — A herdsman roars at comfortable religion that tramples the poor. 8th c. BC. The NT church hears the nations called by his name (Acts 15).

**Obadiah** — Shortest OT book. Edom standing aside while Jacob burns. Oldest: Twelve tradition.

**Jonah** — GOLD file already written.

**Micah** — Justice, mercy, humility; a ruler from Bethlehem. 8th c. Matthew hears Bethlehem here.

**Nahum** — Nineveh’s turn. Empires that bleed the world do not get the last word. Pair with Jonah on purpose.

**Habakkuk** — A prophet argues with God and learns to wait, then to sing. Qumran wrote a commentary (*pesher*) on chs. 1–2. Paul hears “the righteous shall live by faith” here.

**Zephaniah** — Day of the LORD against pride; God will rejoice over a remnant. Josiah’s generation.

**Haggai** — Rebuild the house. 520 BC. Named date. Rare and useful.

**Zechariah** — Night visions, a priest-king, a humble king on a donkey. Gospels take the donkey from here.

**Malachi** — Last OT voice in the Protestant order: stop giving leftovers; a messenger is still coming. The Gospels open with that messenger named John.
