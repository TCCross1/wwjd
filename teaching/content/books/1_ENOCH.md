# 1 Enoch
**Shelf:** Tewahedo Scripture · Jewish Second Temple writing · not in Protestant/Catholic/Eastern Orthodox canons  
**Genre:** Apocalypse in five booklets  
**Badge:** Tewahedo

---

## 1. The Door

**One sentence:** A Jewish seer named Enoch — the man Genesis says “walked with God” — is written as the witness of fallen angels, strange calendars, a coming judgment, and a figure called the Son of Man.

**Time ribbon:** Written in pieces from the 3rd century BC into the 1st century BC (the Parables booklet may be later). Complete book kept in Ethiopia.

**Path line:** “Angels cross a line. The world breaks. God still judges. Ethiopia never stopped reading this. Most other churches did.”

---

## 2. The Campfire

Genesis gives Enoch four verses and a disappearance. This book gives him journeys.

The Watchers, angels meant to watch the earth, take human wives. Their children are giants. They teach weapon-making, cosmetics, spells. Blood fills the ground. Enoch is sent with a verdict. Flood is coming. Enoch is also walked through storehouses of stars, winds, and weeks of the world. Animals stand in for empires. A “Son of Man” sits with the Head of Days. The last booklet blesses the righteous and warns the rich who swallow the poor.

It is not a children’s angel book. It is a protest against a violent world, written as vision.

---

## 3. The Passport

**Traditional author (inside the book):** Enoch, seventh from Adam.  
**Historical composition:** Several Jewish writers, mostly in Aramaic, across 300–100 BC (Parables / Similitudes often placed in the 1st c. BC or a little later).  
**Why the name?** Ancient writers often spoke in a father’s voice. That is a genre fact, not a modern insult.

**First audience:** Jews under Greek, then Hasmonean, pressure — people who believed the world was too wrong for ordinary politics to fix.

**Language path:** Aramaic (Qumran) → Greek fragments → **complete Ge’ez**.

**Purpose:** To say evil has a story older than this year’s empire, and that God has already written the end of it.

---

## 4. The World

This is the same century-band as Daniel, Sirach, and the Maccabean wars. People are asking: why do the righteous suffer, and why does heaven feel crowded with rebels? 1 Enoch answers with a mythic history of angelic trespass — which is exactly why later rabbis and bishops grew uneasy.

---

## 5. The Map

Pins: the traditional landing of the Watchers on Mount Hermon; the ends of the earth in the journey cycles (symbolic); Ethiopia as the land that kept the whole book; Qumran as the land that kept the oldest scraps.

---

## 6. How to read it

This is vision, not a newspaper and not Genesis midrash pretending to be Moses.
Read it next to Genesis 5–6 and Jude 14–15.
Do not let internet “forbidden Bible” videos be the teacher.

---

## 7. The Walkthrough (five booklets)

1. **Book of the Watchers** (1–36) — the fall, the flood verdict, Enoch’s tours. Oldest core.
2. **Book of Parables / Similitudes** (37–71) — thrones, the Son of Man. **Not found at Qumran.**
3. **Astronomical Book** (72–82) — a 364-day calendar. Very old. Qumran loved this.
4. **Dream Visions** (83–90) — Animal Apocalypse. History as a flock.
5. **Epistle of Enoch** (91–108) — two ways, woes on the rich, hope for the just.

---

## 8. Two Chairs — why most Bibles do not bind it

**Chair A — Ethiopia and some early Christians (Tertullian loved it; Jude used it).**  
Jude quotes Enoch 1:9 as a true word about judgment. Early fathers used the Watchers story to explain evil. A church that received this with its first Scriptures is not obligated to throw it away because Hippo later made a shorter list.

**Chair B — Synagogue, Rome, Constantinople, Wittenberg.**  
The Hebrew canon never listed it. It is composite and late. It can be read as shifting the origin of sin onto angels in a way Genesis 3 does not. Jerome and Augustine stepped back. No ecumenical council of the Latin or Greek church listed it. Jude can tell the truth with a line without canonizing the whole five-booklet stack.

**Shared ground:** Jude used it. Qumran copied it. The complete book would have vanished without Ethiopia. It is a real ancient Jewish voice, not a medieval fake.

**Not settled by:** “They hid it because of UFOs.”

---

## 9. The Ink / Oldest copy

- Oldest: Aramaic fragments from Qumran, 3rd–1st c. BC, about 11 manuscripts, mostly Watchers, Dreams, Astronomical material.
- Missing at Qumran: the Parables (the Son of Man booklet).
- Complete: Ge’ez manuscripts (the earliest surviving complete copies are medieval).
- Greek: substantial fragments (Chester Beatty / Michigan and others).
- Public-domain English for study: R.H. Charles, 1912.

Compare-care: when someone says “the original Book of Enoch,” ask *which booklet* and *which language*.

---

## 10. Hard Ground

The Watchers and the women. Giant children. Cosmic geography. A Son of Man who, in a late turn of the Parables, can be identified with Enoch himself — which is *not* how the New Testament uses “Son of Man.” Label that difference.

---

## 11. Thread to Jesus

Jude already made the bridge: judgment is coming with the holy ones.
The Gospels’ “Son of Man” language lives in the same Jewish air, especially Daniel 7. That does not make 1 Enoch a fifth Gospel.
Tewahedo reading hears Enoch as part of the first testament that prepares the world for the Judge. Other churches hear it as a powerful cousin of Daniel, not as binding Scripture.

**Remember**
1. 1 Enoch is a library in one name.
2. Qumran had pieces. Ethiopia had the book.
3. Jude’s quote is a door, not a command to bind all 108 chapters.

**Practice**  
Read Genesis 5:21–24 and Jude 14–15, then Enoch 1. Notice what Jude actually uses — and what he does not.
