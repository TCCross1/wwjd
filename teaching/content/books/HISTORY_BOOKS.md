# History books — compact modules
Gold-level twelve rooms still to be expanded later. Every book below already has Door, Passport, Oldest copy, one live chair, Jesus thread.

---

## Joshua
**Door:** The land promised to Abraham is entered. The book is gift, warfare, and a last speech: choose whom you will serve.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Joshua. Many scholars — a Deuteronomistic history shaped later (7th–6th c. BC). Setting: Canaan after Moses. Purpose: the promise has feet on soil, and the people still have to stay loyal.  
**Oldest copy:** 4QJoshᵃ, 2nd–1st c. BC (2 copies at Qumran).  
**Chair:** Holy war as written vs. a later theological portrait of settlement. Shared ground: the book itself ends with “we will serve the LORD,” not with a body count as the point.  
**Jesus thread:** Jesus is named Yehoshua. Hebrews 4 treats Joshua’s rest as unfinished.

## Judges
**Door:** Without a faithful center, Israel loops: sin, misery, cry, a deliverer, repeat — “everyone did what was right in his own eyes.”  
**Passport:** Tradition — Samuel or circles around him. Scholars — gathered stories inside the Deuteronomistic history. Dates of events: Iron Age; book finished later.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 1st c. BC (3 copies).  
**Chair:** History of a messy period vs. a sermon in cycles. Both can be true.  
**Jesus thread:** The book begs for a true king and a true judge. Ruth will open a door toward David; the Gospels open a door beyond David.

## Ruth
**Door:** In the dark days of the judges, a Moabite woman’s loyalty becomes a road to David.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Samuel. Date of story: judges era. Date of writing: disputed (from David’s court to after exile).  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 1st c. BC (4 copies).  
**Chair:** Sweet idyll vs. a sharp argument that a foreigner belongs in Israel’s royal line.  
**Jesus thread:** Matthew’s family list of Jesus names Ruth on purpose.

## 1 Samuel
**Door:** A prophet, a tall king, and a shepherd — Israel learns what kind of ruler it asked for.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Samuel, then Nathan and Gad. Scholars — compiled court history.  
**Oldest copy:** 4QSamᵃ among the earliest biblical scrolls (~250–200 BC). 4 copies of Samuel.  
**Ink:** 4QSamᵃ and the Greek often help where the medieval Hebrew is difficult (the height of Goliath, the story of Nahash, plus and minus verses).  
**Jesus thread:** “Son of David” starts here as a hope with a human face.

## 2 Samuel
**Door:** David reigns, sins, and is still held by a promise that will outlive him.  
**Passport:** Same as 1 Samuel; they were one book in Hebrew.  
**Oldest copy:** same Samuel scrolls.  
**Hard ground:** Bathsheba, Uriah, Amnon, Absalom. Do not tidy the text.  
**Jesus thread:** 2 Samuel 7 is the load-bearing wall under every later “Messiah” sentence.

## 1 Kings
**Door:** Wisdom on a throne, a temple built, then a kingdom split by pride.  
**Passport:** Anonymous court/Deuteronomistic historian. Finished in exile (6th c. BC) in the common critical view; conservative readers still hear sources from the palaces themselves.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS Kings fragments, 2nd–1st c. BC (3).  
**Jesus thread:** The temple Jesus walks into is this building’s grandchild. “Something greater than Solomon is here.”

## 2 Kings
**Door:** Israel falls to Assyria, Judah to Babylon. The prophets had said it aloud.  
**Passport:** Same historian as 1 Kings. Ends in exile with a Davidic king at a foreign table — a sliver of hope.  
**Oldest copy:** same.  
**Jesus thread:** Exile is the wound Matthew’s first pages assume. Jesus is born under another empire.

## 1 Chronicles
**Door:** After disaster, a writer retells the story around worship, priests, and David.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Ezra. Date often 5th–4th c. BC. Purpose: a shattered people still have a liturgy and a line.  
**Oldest copy:** 4Q118, 1st c. BC (1 copy). Thin.  
**Chair:** “Whitewashed David” vs. “pastoral retelling for a community that already knows 2 Samuel.”  
**Jesus thread:** Genealogies in Chronicles are why Matthew and Luke can still name a line.

## 2 Chronicles
**Door:** Judah’s kings judged by whether they sought the LORD. The last word is a Persian edict: you may go home.  
**Passport:** Same as 1 Chronicles. Prayer of Manasseh is tucked into this story-world in some traditions (Tewahedo / Orthodox).  
**Oldest copy:** with 1 Chronicles.  
**Jesus thread:** “Go up” at the end is the shape of every later return — including the one the Gospels call kingdom.

## Ezra
**Door:** A remnant comes home to raise an altar and a people around the book of the Law.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Ezra. Date of events: late 6th–5th c. BC.  
**Oldest copy:** 4Q117 (Ezra), 1st c. BC.  
**Chair:** Heroic reformer vs. a harder look at who got sent away in the mixed-marriage crisis.  
**Jesus thread:** A people formed by a book. Jesus will claim to fulfill that book, not junk it.

## Nehemiah
**Door:** Walls rise in spite of mockery. The harder work is a city that remembers who it is.  
**Passport:** First-person memoir plus editing. Often paired with Ezra as one work in Hebrew counting.  
**Oldest copy:** no clear stand-alone Qumran Nehemiah. Medieval MT / Greek Bibles.  
**Jesus thread:** Rebuilding a city is not the same as the city John sees coming down — but the hunger is the same.

## Esther
**Door:** In Persia, with God’s name never printed, a hidden providence keeps a people alive.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Mordecai. Date: Persian setting; writing often placed 4th–3rd c. BC.  
**Oldest copy:** **No DSS copy.** Greek Esther (longer, with prayers and God’s name restored) appears in papyri and the great 4th-c. codices. Hebrew Esther’s complete form is medieval MT.  
**Chairs:** Why is God unnamed? Why is Esther missing at Qumran (chance vs. calendar/feast tension vs. taste)? Greek additions: Catholic/Orthodox keep them; Protestants usually print the shorter Hebrew story.  
**Jesus thread:** Providence that hides is still providence. The book is about a people not being erased.
