# Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy
Genesis and Exodus have gold files. These three complete the Torah spine.

---

## Leviticus
**Door:** A holy God teaches a rescued people how to live near Him without being consumed.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Moses. Scholars — priestly instruction gathered over time, final form often placed in the exile or after. Setting: Sinai camp. Purpose: holiness is not mood. It is how a dangerous mercy is housed.  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 3rd–2nd c. BC; 11QpaleoLev; ~13–15 copies. 4QLXXLevᵃ 2nd/1st c. BC.  
**How to read:** Ritual is theology you can touch. Do not skip the blood as if it were embarrassment, and do not play priest at home.  
**Hard ground:** sacrifice, skin disease, sexual holiness, “abomination” lists. Two chairs: lasting moral grain vs. Israel’s specific purity map fulfilled/transformed in Jesus. Shared ground: God is not safe and God wants to dwell.  
**Jesus thread:** The whole book is why “Lamb of God” means something. Hebrews walks this tent on purpose.

## Numbers
**Door:** The wilderness becomes a long test. Will the rescued people trust, or will they die circling?  
**Passport:** Tradition — Moses. Name in Hebrew: “In the wilderness.”  
**Oldest copy:** DSS 2nd–1st c. BC (~8–11). **Ketef Hinnom (~600 BC)** quotes the priestly blessing of 6:24–26 — oldest biblical words we have.  
**Walkthrough:** census → Sinai departure → complaints → spies → a generation dies → a new census → the edge of the land. Balaam. Cities of refuge.  
**Jesus thread:** John 3 points at the bronze serpent. 1 Corinthians 10 treats these stories as warnings with a Christ-shaped end.

## Deuteronomy
**Door:** Moses preaches the covenant one last time on the edge of the land: choose life.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Moses (with a postscript on his death). Scholars — the core of Josiah’s reform book (2 Kings 22) and the spine of the Deuteronomistic history; 7th c. BC for much of the preaching form. Both chairs.  
**Oldest copy:** among the earliest DSS (~250–200 BC). Most-copied Torah book (~30–35). 4QDeutⁿ (Ten Words). Greek: P.Rylands 458, 2nd c. BC.  
**Ink:** 32:8 sons of Israel (MT) vs. sons of God (LXX + DSS). The Shema (6:4–5) is the heart Jesus names as first command.  
**Jesus thread:** Jesus answers the devil with Deuteronomy three times. The law He fulfills is this preached covenant, not a slogan.
