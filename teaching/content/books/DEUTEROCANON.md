# Deuterocanon / Protestant Apocrypha
**Badge:** Catholic + Orthodox (+ Tewahedo except Greek Maccabees)

Who set these aside as dogma: rabbinic Judaism; Protestant churches (many still printed them as a section for centuries).
Who keeps them as Scripture: Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Tewahedo (most titles).
Reasons: see `07_CANON_WHO_LEFT_WHAT_OUT.md`.

---

## Tobit
**Door:** A blind father, a haunted bride, an angel in disguise — mercy in the diaspora.  
**Date:** ~225–175 BC.  
**Oldest copy:** 5 Qumran mss, 4 Aramaic + 1 Hebrew. Before 1947 we mostly had Greek.  
**Purpose:** God still sees scattered families.  
**Jesus thread:** Almsgiving, angels, honoring parents — the air of Jesus’ Judaism.

## Judith
**Door:** A widow saves her people by walking into an enemy general’s tent.  
**Date:** ~150–100 BC. Historical dress is loose (Nebuchadnezzar as “Assyrian,” etc.).  
**Oldest copy:** Greek tradition; no DSS.  
**Chair:** History vs. deliverance-tale. Shared ground: the point is courage and the God of Israel, not a war college text.

## Additions to Esther
**Door:** Greek Esther puts back the prayers and the name of God that Hebrew Esther withholds.  
**Oldest copy:** Greek papyri and the great codices.  
**Chair:** Original story as the short Hebrew vs. a diaspora edition that could not bear the silence.

## Wisdom of Solomon
**Door:** A Greek-speaking Jew praises Wisdom as God’s partner and preaches that the souls of the just are in God’s hand.  
**Date:** ~100–50 BC, likely Alexandria.  
**Oldest copy:** Greek; no DSS.  
**Jesus thread:** Language of Wisdom and the righteous sufferer runs near the Gospels and Hebrews. Paul and John live in related air.

## Sirach (Ecclesiasticus, Ben Sira)
**Door:** A Jerusalem teacher writes a handbook for a faithful life — honor, speech, friends, the fear of the Lord.  
**Date:** ~180 BC; grandson translated it into Greek ~132 BC.  
**Oldest copy:** Hebrew at Qumran, Masada, and later Cairo Genizah. We can now check Greek against Hebrew.  
**Why Protestants set it aside:** not in the Hebrew 24 as a closed list; late; wisdom, not prophecy.  
**Why others keep it:** synagogue and church taught from it for centuries; Athanasius still said “read it.”

## Baruch + Letter of Jeremiah
**Door:** Exiles are told how to pray in a foreign city, and why idols are a joke that kills.  
**Letter of Jeremiah oldest copy:** 7Q2 Greek, 2nd/1st c. BC.  
**Baruch:** Greek / Latin tradition.

## 1 Maccabees
**Door:** A history of the Jewish revolt against Antiochus IV — temple recovered, Hanukkah’s root.  
**Date:** ~100 BC. Hebrew original lost; we have Greek.  
**Not in Tewahedo** (they read Meqabyan instead).  
**Why some keep it:** it is how we know the revolt.  
**Why some will not found dogma on it:** late, political, no clear prophetic voice.

## 2 Maccabees
**Door:** The same crisis as theology — martyrs, resurrection hope, prayer for the dead.  
**Date:** ~124 BC or a little after, a Greek abridgment of Jason of Cyrene.  
**This is the flashpoint book.** Protestants point here when they say the extra books were used for purgatory. Catholics point here when they say resurrection hope and intercession are already Jewish.  
Two Chairs, full strength, no sneer.

## 1 Esdras (Greek Esdras / Tewahedo 2 Izra)
Parallel to 2 Chronicles–Ezra plus the tale of the three guardsmen. Orthodox + Tewahedo.

## 3 Maccabees
Jews in Ptolemaic Egypt threatened and delivered. Orthodox. Not the Ethiopian Meqabyan.

## Psalm 151
David the overlooked shepherd. **Hebrew in 11Q5.** Greek in LXX. Orthodox + Tewahedo. Qumran ended the “this is only a late Greek poem” shrug.

## Prayer of Manasseh
A wicked king’s repentance. Orthodox; Tewahedo reads it with Chronicles; many Protestant apocrypha sections printed it.

## Additions to Daniel
Song of the Three, Susanna, Bel and the Dragon. Greek (and other). Catholic/Orthodox/Tewahedo. Oldest path is Greek, not Qumran Hebrew Daniel (which follows the shorter shape).
