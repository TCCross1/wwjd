# Exodus
**Section:** Torah  
**Genre:** Covenant story + law  
**Default map:** Exodus and Wilderness

---

## 1. The Door

**One sentence:** God hears slaves, names Himself, breaks an empire, and makes a free people into a worshiping nation at a mountain.

**Image-idea:** Night in Egypt. A doorframe marked. A table inside. Sandals already on.

**Time ribbon:** From a family in Egypt to a people at Sinai — the rescue every later book keeps remembering.

**Path line:** “Israel is stuck in Egypt. God sends Moses. The sea opens. God gives a way to live with Him.”

---

## 2. The Campfire

### Path
Jacob’s family has grown in Egypt and become slaves. Pharaoh fears them and tries to kill the boys. A Hebrew baby floats in a basket, is pulled out by Pharaoh’s daughter, and named Moses. Grown Moses kills an Egyptian, runs to Midian, and meets God in a bush that burns but is not eaten. God says: I have heard. I AM. Go.

Plagues follow a hard heart. The last night is Passover — lamb, blood on the door, a meal in a hurry. Israel walks out. Pharaoh chases. The sea splits. On the far side they sing. In the wilderness they complain and are fed. At Sinai God speaks ten words and many more. They build a golden calf while Moses is still on the mountain. God does not abandon them. They build a tent so the Holy One can live in the camp. Exodus ends with glory filling that tent.

### Trail
Exodus is the gospel shape of the Old Testament: bondage, a mediator, blood, a path through death-water, a covenant, a dwelling. Law here is not the opposite of mercy. Law arrives after rescue. You do not earn the sea opening. You learn how to live as free people who belong to the God who opened it.

Two geographies matter: Egypt’s brick-yards and Sinai’s thin air. Two names matter: Pharaoh, who will not know the LORD, and YHWH, who will be known.

---

## 3. The Passport

**Traditional author:** Moses, with the people, during the wilderness years.  
**Other serious proposals:** Core memory of a real departure, written and gathered over centuries; priestly shaping of the tabernacle chapters; final form in or after exile when “leave the empire, become a holy people” was needed again.

**Traditional date of events:** Often 1446 BC (1 Kings 6:1 counted backward) or a 13th-century Ramesses setting (store-cities named in 1:11).  
**Academic range for the book as we have it:** Many place the finished form in the first millennium BC, with older songs and stories inside (the sea song in chapter 15 is often called very old).  
**Why dates differ:** Archaeology, Egyptian records, and the Bible’s own numbers are read more than one honest way. Say so.

**Setting:** Egypt, the wilderness of the south, Mount Sinai (traditional: Jebel Musa in the south Sinai; other proposals exist — mark **disputed**).  
**First audience:** Israel — a people who must remember they were slaves, and whose God is not Pharaoh.  
**Language:** Hebrew.  
**Purpose in one line:** To tell how the LORD saved Israel and came to live with them.

---

## 4. The World Around the Words

New Kingdom Egypt was a machine of gods, monuments, and forced labor. A “Pharaoh who did not know Joseph” is how the book says memory died and policy turned cruel. Brick quotas, midwives, a river used as a weapon — this is state power against a people.

Wilderness is not empty in the story. It is the classroom. Water, meat, bread, a mountain, a tent.

## 5. The Map

Pins:
- Goshen — Israel in Egypt
- Nile
- Pithom and Rameses — store-cities; historical identification **disputed**
- Reed Sea / “Red Sea” — crossing site **disputed**; do not drop a confident pin in the Gulf of Suez and call it done
- Marah, Elim, Rephidim
- Sinai / Horeb — **disputed** exact peak
- Midian — Moses’ years as a shepherd

### On the tray
**Find:** Rekhmire’s brickyard painting (Thebes) and Ramesside quota texts (“no straw,” daily brick counts).  
**Feel:** Oppression is mud, a mold, a number, and an overseer.  
**Does not prove:** These workers are the Hebrews of Moses, or which Pharaoh Exodus means.  
**Badge:** neighbor-text · excavated scene

Movement line: Egypt → water → wilderness → mountain → camp with a tent at the center.

## 6. How to Read This Kind of Book

**This genre does:** Tell a rescue and then give the rescued a way of life and worship.  
**This genre does not:** Separate “story chapters” and “boring law chapters” as if God changed books in the middle.

Rules:
1. Rescue first, commands second. That order is theology.
2. The tabernacle chapters are a slow camera on a single claim: God wants to dwell.
3. Pharaoh’s hard heart is a hard ground room. Do not flatten it into a cartoon.

**Common misuse:** Using Exodus only as a political mascot, or skipping to the Ten Commandments as if the lamb and the sea were decoration.

## 8. The People

Moses, Aaron, Miriam, the midwives Shiphrah and Puah, Jethro, Pharaoh, and a people who can sing at the sea and build a calf in the same year.

---

## 9. Two Chairs

### When did the exodus happen, and how large was it?
Chair A: 15th century, early date, large people, miracles as written.  
Chair B: 13th century under Ramesside kings, or a smaller group whose story became Israel’s story.  
Shared ground: Israel’s Bible refuses to start with a throne. It starts with slaves and a God who hears.

### Did God harden Pharaoh, or did Pharaoh harden himself?
The book says both. Chair A emphasizes God’s freedom to judge an empire. Chair B emphasizes Pharaoh’s repeated no. Shared ground: the text will not make Pharaoh a hero and will not make God a nervous spectator.

### How should Christians read the law of Exodus after Jesus?
Chair A: moral law remains, ceremonial law is fulfilled, civil law was for that nation.  
Chair B: the whole covenant is fulfilled in Christ and now teaches as wisdom and as a pointer, not as Israel’s legal code copied into a modern state.  
Shared ground: Jesus does not treat Moses as an enemy. Love of God and neighbor is the law’s own peak.

---

## 10. The Ink

**The Name in 3:14** — “I AM WHO I AM” / “I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE.” Hebrew *Ehyeh asher ehyeh*. God will be God-for-them.  
**“Red Sea”** — Hebrew *yam suph*, “sea of reeds.” English “Red Sea” is old habit through Greek and Latin. The miracle claim does not hang on the English color.  
**“You shall not murder”** — the Hebrew is about unlawful killing, not every taking of life in war or court as ancient Israel knew those. Teach the word carefully.

---

## 11. Hard Ground

The last plague. Herem-language later in the Torah. Why firstborn? Why Egypt’s people under a king they did not elect? Pastoral chair: God breaks a system that was already killing Hebrew sons. Critical chair: the text is still dreadful and must not be used to bless later violence. Keep both. The Church reads this night through a Passover Lamb the New Testament names as Jesus — that is a Christian reading, and it should be labeled as such.

---

## 12. The Thread to Jesus

Jesus’ own Passover night, His “I am” sayings, the baptismal echo of a path through water, John 1:14 — “the Word became flesh and tented among us.” The tabernacle is not a cute metaphor. It is God wanting to live with a people. Christians claim that desire got a human face.

**Remember**
1. Freedom is a gift before it is a lifestyle.
2. The mountain comes after the sea.
3. Glory in a tent is the point of the building plans.

**Practice**  
Read Exodus 3 and 12 in one sitting. Ask: what does God hear, and what does a household have to do to walk out?
