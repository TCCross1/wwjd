# New Testament — remaining compact modules
Mark, Romans, Revelation already have gold files.

---

## Matthew
**Door:** Jesus the son of David and Abraham fulfills Scripture and teaches a kingdom on a mountain.  
**Passport:** Tradition — the apostle Matthew / Levi. Many scholars — a Greek gospel using Mark and a sayings source, 70s–80s AD, a mixed Jewish-Christian church.  
**Oldest copy:** P104, P77, P103, P64/67 (2nd–3rd c.). Full text: Vaticanus, Sinaiticus.  
**How to read:** A teaching Gospel. Five discourse blocks. Fulfillment formulas are a feature, not a glitch.  
**Chair:** Apostle as author vs. a Matthew-school compiling Mark + testimony. Shared ground: this is a first-century church’s Jesus, soaked in Israel’s book.  
**Jesus thread:** The whole book *is* the thread. Sermon on the Mount is WWJD in paragraphs.

## Luke
**Door:** An orderly account for Theophilus: the Savior of the lowly, the lost, and the nations.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Luke the physician, companion of Paul. Date: 70s–90s common; some earlier. Same author as Acts.  
**Oldest copy:** P75 ~200 (most of Luke 3–24), text close to Vaticanus.  
**Map:** Nazareth to Bethlehem to Jerusalem to Emmaus — then Acts will leave the city.  
**Jesus thread:** Magnificat, prodigal son, the thief on the cross — mercy with a spine.

## John
**Door:** The Word became flesh. Signs, I-am sayings, a glory that looks like a washed foot and a cross.  
**Passport:** Tradition — John the son of Zebedee. Others — a Johannine circle in Asia, late 1st c.  
**Oldest copy:** **P52**, 2nd c., five verses of ch. 18. **P66** ~200 nearly the whole Gospel. P75 John 1–15. P66 and P75 **lack** 7:53–8:11.  
**Ink:** 1:1 “the Word was God”; the woman caught in adultery as a beloved early story with a late manuscript pedigree — treat like Mark 16.  
**Jesus thread:** “Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.”

## Acts
**Door:** The risen Jesus keeps working by the Spirit as the news moves from Jerusalem toward Rome.  
**Passport:** Volume two of Luke.  
**Oldest copy:** P45 (3rd); 0189 (2nd/3rd, parchment).  
**Map:** Jerusalem → Samaria → Antioch → Asia → Greece → Rome. This is a movement map, not a closed book.  
**Chair:** History vs. preached history. Shared ground: the church is not an accident.

---

## 1 Corinthians
**Door:** A gifted, divided church is called back to the cross and to love that does not puff up.  
**Passport:** Paul, from Ephesus, ~AD 54–55. Undisputed.  
**Oldest copy:** P46 ~200.  
**Hard ground:** spiritual gifts, sex, head coverings, resurrection body — teach in context, not as internet grenades.

## 2 Corinthians
**Door:** Ministry that looks like dying, and a treasure in clay jars.  
**Passport:** Paul, ~AD 55–56. Possibly more than one letter stitched.  
**Oldest copy:** P46.

## Galatians
**Door:** Freedom in Christ against any gospel that adds a badge at the door.  
**Passport:** Paul, ~AD 48–55 (South Galatia early date vs. later). Undisputed.  
**Oldest copy:** P46.  
**Chair:** “Works of the law” as moralism vs. as ethnic badges. Same fight as Romans.

## Ephesians
**Door:** One new humanity in Christ, and a household that learns to stand.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Paul from prison. Many scholars — a later Pauline disciple. Date 60s or 80s.  
**Oldest copy:** P46.  
**Chair:** Authorship. Shared ground: the vision of one church from two peoples.

## Philippians
**Door:** Joy from jail, and a hymn about a Lord who went down.  
**Passport:** Paul, prison (Rome or Ephesus), 50s–60s. Undisputed.  
**Oldest copy:** P46.  
**Ink:** 2:6–11 may be an early hymn Paul quotes.

## Colossians
**Door:** Christ above every power; do not be kidnapped by spiritual fashion.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Paul. Disputed by some on style.  
**Oldest copy:** P46.

## 1 Thessalonians
**Door:** A young church waiting for Jesus, told to keep working and hoping.  
**Passport:** Paul, ~AD 50. Likely the oldest surviving Christian letter. Undisputed.  
**Oldest copy:** P46 (1 Thess survives there; 2 Thess less so).

## 2 Thessalonians
**Door:** Calm down about rumors of the end; stand firm.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Paul. Some scholars — later, because the timetable feels different from 1 Thess.  
**Oldest copy:** thinner than 1 Thess in the early papyri.

## 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy, Titus
**Door:** Care for a household of faith — teaching, character, order; then a last charge: fan the gift, come before winter.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Paul to coworkers. Many scholars — Pauline school, 80s–100s, because the church looks more structured. Both chairs.  
**Oldest copy:** P32 (Titus) 2nd–3rd c. **Not in P46.** Vaticanus lacks the Pastorals. Sinaiticus has them.  
**Hard ground:** household codes and “I do not permit…” — Two Chairs required, steel-man both.

## Philemon
**Door:** A runaway slave, a prison cell, and a request that love do what law could demand.  
**Passport:** Paul, undisputed.  
**Oldest copy:** 4th-c. great Bibles (not in surviving P46 pages).  
**Jesus thread:** A man sent back “no longer as a slave, but a brother.” The letter is short and heavy.

---

## Hebrews
**Door:** Jesus is better — priest, sacrifice, and the opened way — so do not drift.  
**Passport:** Author unnamed. Old Western guess — Paul. Origen: only God knows. Date before or after 70 (temple language).  
**Oldest copy:** P46 already includes Hebrews with Paul.  
**Chair:** Pauline vs. a brilliant independent preacher (Barnabas, Apollos, Priscilla have all been guessed). Shared ground: first-century, Scripture-soaked, Christ the priest.

## James
**Door:** Faith that does not work is not faith. Wisdom for tongues, money, and the poor.  
**Passport:** Tradition — James the Lord’s brother. Luther stumbled here.  
**Oldest copy:** P20, P23, 3rd c.  
**Chair:** Against Paul vs. against a cheap Paul. Shared ground: Abraham in both letters.

## 1 Peter
**Door:** Exiles in the empire, living holy lives toward a living hope.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Peter via Silvanus. Some scholars — later, better Greek.  
**Oldest copy:** P72 (with 2 Peter and Jude).

## 2 Peter
**Door:** Remember what you heard; false teachers will not get the last word.  
**Passport:** Most disputed NT letter in modern study (style vs. 1 Peter). Tradition — Peter near death.  
**Oldest copy:** P72.  
**Honesty:** put the authorship chair on the Passport. Do not hide it and do not sneer at the church’s memory.

## 1 John
**Door:** Tests of a living faith: truth about Jesus, love for brothers, a clean walk.  
**Passport:** Johannine circle / the elder.  
**Oldest copy:** early papyri (P9 and others).  
**Ink:** 5:7 KJV “three that bear record in heaven” — the Comma Johanneum — late Latin, not early Greek. Teach it calmly.

## 2 John / 3 John
**Door:** Walk in love and do not host a teaching that guts the incarnation; welcome workers, do not imitate the man who loves to be first.  
**Passport:** “The elder.” Tiny letters, huge pastoral load.

## Jude
**Door:** Contend for the faith; keep yourselves in the love of God.  
**Passport:** Jude, brother of James. Uses 1 Enoch and a Moses-legend (Assumption / Testament of Moses tradition).  
**Oldest copy:** P72.  
**Chair:** Does quoting Enoch canonize Enoch? Jude says the quoted line is true. Ethiopia says the book belongs. Most churches say the quote is enough.
