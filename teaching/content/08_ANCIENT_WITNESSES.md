# Ancient witnesses
## What humanity actually has, and how the app may compare texts

The user asked for “the most ancient text available to compare.”
This file is the truth-layer for that feature.

We do **not** possess the first copy of any biblical book. We possess copies of copies, plus a few inscriptions that quote a passage. The honest miracle is how much survived. The honest limit is how fragmentary most of it is.

---

## The comparison rule (non-negotiable)

The app may put short readings side by side.
It may not paste a whole modern copyrighted translation of a Dead Sea Scroll and call it “the original Bible.”

Allowed on a Compare pane:
- A manuscript ID and date range
- Language
- How much of the book survives in that copy
- A short public-domain English line (KJV; Brenton’s 19th-c. LXX; R.H. Charles 1912 for Enoch/Jubilees where needed)
- A one-sentence note when that copy disagrees with the medieval Hebrew or with a common English Bible
- A link-out label: “See the official plate” (Leon Levy DSS Digital Library, Codex Sinaiticus Project, etc.)

Not allowed:
- Inventing Hebrew that is not in the fragment
- Filling gaps and hiding the brackets
- Calling Ketef Hinnom “the original Torah”
- Dating P52 to “AD 90” as a fact (the range is wider than old textbooks said)

## Families of text the user should meet

### Hebrew and related
| Witness | What it is | Date of the copies we have | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ketef Hinnom silver amulets** | Two tiny rolled silver sheets from a Jerusalem tomb | late 7th / early 6th c. BC | Oldest *surviving biblical wording* — a form of the priestly blessing (Numbers 6:24–26), plus Deuteronomy-like phrases. Not a book. A quote worn on the body. |
| **Dead Sea Scrolls (DSS)** | ~230 biblical mss from Qumran and nearby, plus many non-biblical | ~250 BC – AD 68 | Oldest copies of nearly every Hebrew Bible book except Esther (none found) and possibly Nehemiah as a separate book. Some proto-Masoretic, some closer to the Greek, some their own way. |
| **Nash Papyrus** | Hebrew liturgical sheet from Egypt | ~2nd–1st c. BC | Ten Commandments + Shema. Before 1947 this was the oldest Hebrew biblical ms known. |
| **Samaritan Pentateuch** | First five books as the Samaritan community kept them | medieval copies of a much older tradition | Sometimes agrees with LXX / DSS against later Jewish text. |
| **Masoretic Text (MT)** | Medieval standardized Hebrew with vowels and notes | Aleppo Codex ~AD 920 (now incomplete); Leningrad Codex AD 1008 (complete) | The OT behind almost every modern Protestant Bible. Extraordinarily careful. Still one stream, not the only ancient stream. |
| **Cairo Genizah** | Hundreds of thousands of fragments from a Cairo synagogue storeroom | mostly medieval | A second mountain of Hebrew evidence. |

Esther has not been identified among the Qumran biblical scrolls. That may be chance, or it may say something about that community’s calendar and taste. Do not preach either guess as fact.

Nehemiah: often bundled with Ezra. 4Q117 is Ezra. A stand-alone Nehemiah at Qumran is not established.

### Greek Old Testament
| Witness | What it is | Date | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Septuagint (LXX)** | Jewish translation begun in Alexandria, 3rd–1st c. BC | fragments from 2nd c. BC (P.Rylands 458 / Rahlfs 957 = Deuteronomy; 4QLXXLev, 4QLXXDeut) | Bible of many early Christians. NT writers quote it. Sometimes shorter or longer than MT (Jeremiah). |
| **Greek Minor Prophets scroll (8ḤevXIIgr)** | Greek Twelve from Naḥal Ḥever | ~50 BC–AD 50 | A Greek text already being revised toward Hebrew. |
| **Codex Vaticanus (B)** | Nearly complete Greek Bible | early 4th c. AD | Oldest great one-volume Bible we have. |
| **Codex Sinaiticus (א)** | Greek Bible + Barnabas + Hermas | mid 4th c. AD | Oldest *complete* NT. Shows a 4th-century church binding extra books after Revelation. |
| **Codex Alexandrinus (A)** | Greek Bible | 5th c. AD | Another early pandect. |

### Aramaic and Syriac
- **Targums** — Aramaic synagogue renderings, interpretive.
- **Peshitta** — Syriac church Bible. Important for both testaments.
- **Job Targum at Qumran** — Aramaic Job among the scrolls.

### Latin
- **Old Latin** — translations before Jerome, often from Greek.
- **Vulgate** — Jerome’s Latin, OT mostly from Hebrew. Shaped the Western church for a thousand years.

### Ge’ez (Ethiopic)
- The Tewahedo Bible lives in Ge’ez.
- For 1 Enoch and Jubilees, Ge’ez is how the *complete* books survived. Qumran holds the older fragments; Ethiopia holds the whole river.

## The oldest wording we have, period

1. **Ketef Hinnom** (~600 BC) — priestly blessing.
2. Then a long silence in surviving *manuscripts*.
3. **Earliest DSS biblical fragments** — mid-3rd century BC (some Exodus, Samuel, Jeremiah, Deuteronomy pieces).
4. **Great Isaiah Scroll** — ~125 BC, a whole prophetic book you can still unroll in photographs.
5. **Earliest LXX fragments** — 2nd century BC Deuteronomy and Leviticus.
6. **Earliest NT** — 2nd-century scraps of John and (probably) Matthew and Revelation; substantial books around AD 200.

That is the real ladder. Teach it. Do not flatten it into “we found the original.”

## How a Compare pane should look (one verse)

Example: Deuteronomy 32:8

| Column | Text |
|---|---|
| Masoretic / most English Bibles | “sons of Israel” |
| LXX + some DSS | “sons of God” (divine council) |
| Why it matters | One reading is ethnic; the other is cosmic. |
| Not in dispute | The Most High assigned nations their place. |

Example: Isaiah 7:14

| Column | Text |
|---|---|
| Hebrew *almah* | young woman |
| Greek *parthenos* | virgin |
| Matthew | reads the Greek and applies it to Mary |
| Not in dispute | Isaiah gave a sign to Ahaz; the NT hears a further fulfillment |

Example: Mark 16:8 / 16:9–20

| Column | Text |
|---|---|
| Vaticanus, Sinaiticus | stop at 16:8 |
| Majority of later mss | include 16:9–20 |
| App behavior | print both, label the long ending |
