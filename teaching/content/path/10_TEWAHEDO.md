# Path library — Tewahedo-distinct
These rooms are home for Ethiopian users, not a museum.

## 1 Enoch
**Door:** Watchers cross a line, a righteous man is shown the world, and judgment is written in heaven.  
**Campfire:** Angels take wives. Violence fills the earth. Enoch walks. Books of parables, luminaries, dreams. A coming figure some churches hear as the Son of Man.  
**Ink:** Watchers and more in Aramaic at Qumran. The Parables / Son of Man booklet is not in those caves. Ethiopia kept the long book.  
**Chairs:** Second Temple scripture-adjacent vs. later church Scripture in Ethiopia. Jude quotes a line. That is not the same as a 66-book canon.  
**Watch:** No “forbidden book” videos.  
**Thread:** Jude. Jesus’ “Son of Man” language lives in a wider Second Temple air.  
**Carry:** Heaven keeps books. So should we handle this one with honor, not hype.

## Jubilees
**Door:** Genesis and Exodus retold with a 364-day clock and a concern for calendar holiness.  
**Campfire:** Angel dictation on Sinai in the book’s own frame. Weeks of years. Ancestors kept Torah before Sinai.  
**Object:** Hebrew Jubilees at Qumran.  
**Thread:** A people fighting to stay distinct under Greek pressure.  
**Carry:** Time itself can be an act of worship.

## 1 Meqabyan
**Door:** An Ethiopian book about kings, idols, and martyr-faith — not the Greek 1 Maccabees under another name.  
**Object:** A Ge’ez manuscript. No DSS twin.  
**Carry:** Different churches kept different wars of the soul on the page.

## 2 Meqabyan
**Door:** More of that Ethiopian Maccabee-house: pride of kings and the fear of God.  
**Carry:** Do not file this under Judas Maccabeus and walk away.

## 3 Meqabyan
**Door:** A third book in the same Ge’ez room.  
**Carry:** Ask a Tewahedo reader how their church hears it. Do not invent.

## 4 Baruch (Paraleipomena Jeremiou)
**Door:** After the fire, Jeremiah and Baruch keep a story going about figs, sleep, and coming home.  
**Object:** 586 and 70 as the wound twice.  
**Carry:** Some sequels are how a people refuse to let the ruins have the last sentence.
