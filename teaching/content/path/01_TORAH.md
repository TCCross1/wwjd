# Path library — Torah
Each book is one complete visit. Trail/Summit may add from gold files.

---

## Genesis
**Genre:** Origin story  
**Door:** Before Israel is a nation, God makes a world, a family breaks, and a promise starts walking.  
**Image:** A well outside a walled city at dusk.  
**Time:** Beginning of the big story.

**Campfire:** God speaks a world into being and calls it good. Humans are given a garden and a name. They reach for a knowledge that splits them from God and from each other. Violence grows until a flood, then a tower, then God calls Abram: leave, and I will make you a blessing. Promise hangs on a barren couple. Isaac is born. Jacob wrestles and becomes Israel. Joseph is sold, then saves the family in Egypt. The book ends with a coffin in a foreign land and a promise still unpaid.

**Passport:** Tradition — Moses. Other — Israel’s later storytellers gathering old family memory. Dates range from Mosaic era to the exile and after. Language: Hebrew. Purpose: this is who we are and whose we are.

**World:** Ancestor road. City-kings, wells, honor, famine that pulls people to Egypt.  
**Fear:** no son, no grave in the land.  
**Object:** Middle Bronze city gate. Does not prove Abraham walked that exact gate.

**Walkthrough:** World made and broken (1–11) → Promise on the road (12–25) → Twins and the new name (25–36) → Joseph and Egypt (37–50).

**Chairs:** Is 1–11 a calendar of days, or a holy telling of who made the world? Both chairs are held by people who pray.  
**Ink:** Oldest wording on silver is later (Ketef Hinnom is Numbers). Genesis itself is known in Dead Sea copies.  
**Thread:** The promise to bless the nations walks all the way to Jesus. John will start with “in the beginning” on purpose.  
**Carry sentence:** The fracture is real, and the promise is older than the fracture.  
**Carry question:** What am I being asked to leave so a blessing can start?

## Exodus
**Genre:** Rescue and covenant  
**Door:** A people cry from brick, God comes down, and they meet Him at a mountain.  
**Image:** Mud molds and heat.  
**Time:** Israel becomes a people.

**Campfire:** A new king in Egypt does not remember Joseph. Quotas rise. Babies are threatened. Moses is pulled from water, runs, meets God in a bush that burns and is not eaten. Ten blows fall on the palace’s gods. Lamb blood marks doors. They walk out. Water opens. In the wilderness they complain and are fed. At Sinai God speaks and they say we will do it. They break it with a calf. God stays. A tent is built so He can live in the middle of a stubborn camp.

**Passport:** Tradition — Moses. Other — a core memory told and written across generations. Date of the exit is disputed (often 15th or 13th century BC, or a later telling of an older exit). Language: Hebrew.

**World:** Empire Egypt. Brick, river, palace gods.  
**Fear:** our sons.  
**Object:** Rekhmire brickyard + quota texts. Does not prove which Pharaoh.

**Walkthrough:** Slavery and a name (1–6) → Contests and exit (7–15) → Wilderness school (16–18) → Mountain and words (19–24) → Tent and a second chance (25–40).

**Chairs:** Early date vs late date vs a smaller group’s exit later told as a nation’s story. The book still does the same work: we were slaves; God brought us out.  
**Ink:** No brick with “Moses” on it. Neighbor texts show quotas and missing straw.  
**Thread:** Jesus keeps Passover and calls Himself the lamb of a new exit.  
**Carry sentence:** Freedom is a road to a mountain, not only a door out of pain.  
**Carry question:** What would I have to put down to walk out?

## Leviticus
**Genre:** Holy-living instruction  
**Door:** A holy God moves into the camp and teaches a people how to live near Him without being ruined.  
**Image:** A four-horned altar in morning smoke.

**Campfire:** Offerings, priests, clean and unclean, a day when one man walks behind a veil with blood, a year of release, a call to be holy because He is. It is not a random rulebook. It is how a rescued people stay close.

**Passport:** Tradition — Moses at Sinai. Other — priestly teaching gathered over time. Language: Hebrew. Purpose: how to live with God in the middle.

**World:** Wilderness camp / later temple.  
**Object:** Four-horned altar. Does not prove this altar was “the” Leviticus altar.  
**Walkthrough:** Offerings (1–7) → Priests (8–10) → Clean life (11–15) → Day of Covering (16) → Holiness in the street (17–27).  
**Chairs:** Every line still binds as written vs. some lines were for that tent and that land.  
**Ink:** Qumran copies; Ketef Hinnom blesses with priestly words.  
**Thread:** Hebrews says the veil and the blood were pointing at a better priest.  
**Carry sentence:** Holiness is nearness, not a score.  
**Carry question:** What in my ordinary week would have to change if God lived in the middle of it?

## Numbers
**Genre:** Wilderness journey  
**Door:** A counted people walk, complain, refuse the land, and a new generation is counted at the door.  
**Image:** Tents in rings, a peak far off.

**Campfire:** They leave Sinai. They want meat. They spy the land and freeze. Forty years eat a generation. Water from rock, a bronze snake, Balaam hired to curse who can only bless. The children stand on the edge and are numbered again.

**Passport:** Tradition — Moses. Other — wilderness memories compiled later. Language: Hebrew.  
**World:** Wilderness camp.  
**Object:** Ketef Hinnom silver blessing (Numbers 6). Does not prove Moses engraved those scrolls.  
**Walkthrough:** Counted and set out (1–10) → Complaint and refusal (11–14) → Years that swallow (15–21) → Blessing that will not curse (22–24) → A new count (26–36).  
**Chairs:** Journal of the road vs. later preaching in story form.  
**Thread:** John lifts the snake: look and live.  
**Carry sentence:** Unbelief can cost a generation, and God still keeps a count.  
**Carry question:** Where am I refusing a land I asked for?

## Deuteronomy
**Genre:** Covenant sermon  
**Door:** An old man on a ridge retells the road and puts life and death in front of a people about to cross.  
**Image:** Moab, river, land.

**Campfire:** Moses preaches. Hear, O Israel. Love the LORD with all you are. Teach these words to children. Blessing and curse. A future prophet like Moses. He dies in sight of the land he will not enter.

**Passport:** Tradition — Moses’ last words. Other — a later covenant sermon in Moses’ voice, often linked to Josiah’s day. Both chairs love the Shema. Language: Hebrew.  
**Object:** Shema on a door / Qumran tefillin. Does not prove the first recitation’s hour.  
**Walkthrough:** Remember (1–4) → Hear and love (5–11) → Life in the land (12–26) → Two ways (27–30) → A song and a grave (31–34).  
**Chairs:** Moses’ own last day vs. a later preacher wearing Moses’ coat.  
**Thread:** Jesus answers the tester with this book. The Shema stays on His lips.  
**Carry sentence:** Love is a command you walk, not a feeling you wait for.  
**Carry question:** What would I write on my door if I meant it?
