# Path library — History

## Joshua
**Door:** The promise gets dirt under it. A people cross a river and learn that the land is gift and battle.  
**Campfire:** Joshua takes Moses’ place. Water piles up. Jericho’s walls are a march and a shout. A family from the city is spared. Ai is a failure first. Lots divide the hills. Joshua says choose this day.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Joshua. Other — later gathering of conquest memories. Dates sit in the early Iron Age if the story is read as chronicle.  
**World:** Hill village beginning.  
**Object:** Collar-rim jar. Does not prove Joshua’s calendar.  
**Walkthrough:** Cross (1–5) → Take (6–12) → Divide (13–22) → Choose (23–24).  
**Chairs:** Fast conquest vs. a long messy settling. Judges already hints the land was not emptied in a weekend.  
**Thread:** The name Joshua is Jesus’ name in Hebrew. Hebrews treats the rest as still open.  
**Carry:** Gift-land still asks for a choice.

## Judges
**Door:** No king. Everyone does what is right in their own eyes, and it is not right.  
**Campfire:** A cycle: they forget, they are crushed, they cry, a judge rises, they forget again. Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah, Samson — rescue that keeps getting smaller and stranger. The last chapters are a cliff.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Samuel’s day. Other — stories from the tribes gathered under later kings.  
**World:** Hill village. Fear: raid and drought.  
**Object:** Grindstone. Does not prove pig-bone theories by themselves.  
**Walkthrough:** Cycle (1–3) → Heroes (4–16) → Collapse (17–21).  
**Chairs:** History of a dark age vs. a sermon against having no king.  
**Thread:** The book is hungry for a true king. That hunger does not end at David.  
**Carry:** Rescue without a changed heart is only a pause.

## Ruth
**Door:** In the days when judges judged, kindness walks home from Moab.  
**Campfire:** Famine. A grave in a foreign field. Ruth will not let go of Naomi. She gleans. Boaz covers her. A sandal in the gate. A child who will be David’s grandfather.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Samuel. Other — a later story about a Moabite in David’s line.  
**World:** Village harvest.  
**Object:** Saddle quern.  
**Walkthrough:** Loss (1) → Field (2) → Floor (3) → Gate (4).  
**Chairs:** Sweet romance vs. a hard law-and-land story. Both can be true.  
**Thread:** Matthew keeps Ruth in Jesus’ list on purpose.  
**Carry:** Loyal love can change a family name.

## 1 Samuel
**Door:** A boy hears a name in the night, a king is chosen, and another king is anointed who is still a shepherd.  
**Campfire:** Hannah prays. Samuel listens. Israel wants a king like the nations. Saul is tall and then afraid. David is small and then hunted. Jonathan loves him. A cave, a cut robe, a witch at Endor. Saul falls.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Samuel and later prophets. Other — court stories compiled.  
**Object:** Shiloh jar.  
**Walkthrough:** Samuel (1–7) → Saul (8–15) → David rises (16–31).  
**Chairs:** Pro-king vs. anti-king voices stitched together. The book lets both speak.  
**Thread:** A shepherd-king after God’s heart is a shape Jesus will fill and break.  
**Carry:** Wanting a king is not the same as wanting God.

## 2 Samuel
**Door:** The shepherd gets the city, then a roof, then a cry on a stair.  
**Campfire:** David reigns. The ark comes in dancing. Bathsheba. Nathan’s story. Absalom. “O my son.” A last song.  
**Passport:** Court history. Traditional link to prophetic writers.  
**Object:** Tel Dan “House of David.” Does not prove a palace under today’s ridge.  
**Walkthrough:** Rise (1–10) → Fall in the house (11–12) → Sword in the house (13–20) → Last words (21–24).  
**Chairs:** Hero-king vs. a warning about power. The book is both.  
**Thread:** The promise of an forever house over David’s line is the road to a son of David.  
**Carry:** Getting the throne is not the same as keeping a heart.

## 1 Kings
**Door:** Wisdom asks for a listening heart, then luxury and other gods eat the kingdom in two.  
**Campfire:** Solomon prays. A temple. Queen of Sheba. Then horses, gold, and wives who turn him. The kingdom splits. Elijah stands on Carmel. A still small voice.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Jeremiah’s circle. Other — royal records edited in exile.  
**Object:** Samaria ivories. No Solomon temple floor.  
**Walkthrough:** Solomon (1–11) → Split (12–16) → Elijah (17–22).  
**Chairs:** Golden age then fall vs. a story written to explain the fall.  
**Thread:** Jesus says something greater than Solomon is here.  
**Carry:** A listening heart can still drown in gold.

## 2 Kings
**Door:** Prophets and bad kings until Assyria takes the north and Babylon burns the south.  
**Campfire:** Elisha’s wonders. Jehu’s blood. Hezekiah’s tunnel. Josiah finds a book. Then the end: 722 and 586. A king eats rations in Babylon.  
**Passport:** Same as Kings. Exile is the desk.  
**Object:** Lachish letter + Sennacherib relief.  
**Walkthrough:** North dies (1–17) → Judah’s last light (18–23) → Burn (24–25).  
**Chairs:** Straight chronicle vs. theology of why the city fell.  
**Thread:** The line of David goes to Babylon alive. Matthew will start there.  
**Carry:** Finding the book late is still mercy.

## 1 Chronicles
**Door:** After the fire, someone counts the families and retells David as a singer and a temple-planner.  
**Campfire:** Long lists. David wanted a house for God. Music. Gatekeepers. Less Bathsheba, more worship.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Ezra. Other — a post-exilic teacher.  
**Object:** Yehud stamp.  
**Walkthrough:** Names (1–9) → David the liturgist (10–29).  
**Chairs:** Whitewash vs. a people who need hope more than another scolding.  
**Thread:** The son of David theme is turned toward worship.  
**Carry:** Memory can rebuild a people.

## 2 Chronicles
**Door:** The temple’s life, the good kings’ prayers, and a fall that still ends with a pagan king saying go home.  
**Campfire:** Solomon’s house. Reforms. Hezekiah’s water. Josiah. Fire. Cyrus.  
**Object:** Siloam inscription.  
**Walkthrough:** Temple (1–9) → Two ways of kings (10–36).  
**Thread:** The last word is return. The big story is not finished at the ash.  
**Carry:** Even an emperor can be used to send you home.

## Ezra
**Door:** Paper from a far king, a small altar, tears when the old men see the new house.  
**Campfire:** Cyrus says go. Zerubbabel builds. Enemies write letters. Ezra comes with a book and makes a hard call about marriages.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Ezra the scribe. Other — compiled Persian-period records.  
**Object:** Cyrus Cylinder is not Ezra 1 word-for-word. Yehud seals are the small-province weather.  
**Walkthrough:** Return and altar (1–6) → Book and reform (7–10).  
**Chairs:** Heroic rebuild vs. a painful purity fight. Hold both.  
**Thread:** A people of the Book is being born. Jesus will read in that stream.  
**Carry:** Rebuilding is smaller than memory, and still holy.

## Nehemiah
**Door:** A cupbearer asks for stones, and a wall goes up with a trumpet in one hand.  
**Campfire:** News of a broken city. Night ride. Mockers. Built in 52 days. The book is read. Confession. Then the same old sins sneak back.  
**Object:** Elephantine letter — Jews writing petitions in the same empire. Not Nehemiah’s mail.  
**Walkthrough:** Wall (1–7) → Book (8–10) → The work is not finished in the heart (11–13).  
**Thread:** A city and a word together. Jesus will weep over this city later.  
**Carry:** A finished wall is not a finished people.

## Esther
**Door:** A Jewish girl in a Persian court, God’s name never printed, a people almost erased.  
**Campfire:** A queen is dismissed. Esther is taken in. Mordecai will not bow. Haman builds a gallows. A forgotten book is read at night. Tables turn. Purim.  
**Passport:** Tradition — Mordecai’s circle. Other — a later diaspora tale set in Xerxes’ world.  
**Object:** Persepolis tablets, Susa palace. No queen named Esther on them.  
**Walkthrough:** Banquet and plot (1–4) → Risk (5–7) → Deliverance (8–10).  
**Chairs:** History vs. a crafted novella. Either way the feast is real in Jewish life.  
**Thread:** A hidden God and a people kept alive — the line Jesus is born from stays in the world.  
**Carry:** Silence on the page is not absence in the story.
