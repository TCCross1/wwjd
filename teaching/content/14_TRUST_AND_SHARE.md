# Trust and share
## What would make Christians refuse this house — and how we build it so they want to hand it to someone they love

This file is a pastoral risk map, not a list of doctrines we must pick.
The House already tells the truth about chairs, ink, and dirt.
Truth told in the wrong order, with the wrong words, still drives people out.

The goal is not “offend no one.”
The goal is: a grandmother can send this to her grandson without fearing we will unmake his faith, and a skeptic can stay in the room without feeling handled.

---

## 1. The real fear under every objection

Almost every Christian objection is one sentence:

**“Are you trying to take my Bible away from me?”**

If the answer feels like yes — even if we never said it — they will not share us.
If the answer is visibly no — *we are trying to give your Bible back to you, thicker, older, more Jesus-shaped* — they will.

Secondary fears:
- You will make my teenager a skeptic.
- You are sneering at my church.
- You replaced the Holy Spirit with “scholars.”
- You turned Jesus into a bracelet slogan.
- You put someone else’s canon over mine.
- You are fighting the culture war from the other side.

Design for those fears. Do not design for winning an argument on Twitter.

---

## 2. Who will push back, and why

### A. Inerrantist evangelicals and many Baptists
**Trigger:** late dates printed as fact; “legend / myth / invented”; Genesis 1–11 treated as disposable; Jesus thread that never says sin, cross, or risen body; Two Chairs that sound like “pick your own truth.”

**What they will say:** “This undermines the Word. BibleProject already drifted. We will not hand our kids a second version of that.”

**What they need to hear first:** *We receive these books as Holy Scripture. God used human hands. Disagreement is not a hole in the page.*

### B. KJV-only / Textus Receptus / “God preserved every jot”
**Trigger:** Mark ending at 16:8 presented as “the real Bible”; calling the longer ending “fake”; “oldest is best” as a creed.

**What they will say:** “You crowned Vaticanus and dethroned the church’s Bible.”

**What they need:** the longer ending treated as *beloved and widely received*, not as a dirty secret. Show the manuscript map. Do not preach Westcott and Hort as apostles.

### C. Confessional Reformed / Lutheran
**Trigger:** no doctrine of Scripture; canon wing that sounds like “the church invented the list”; Jesus thread that skips law/gospel or justification.

**What they need:** a plain sentence that this tool does not replace pulpit, creed, or catechism.

### D. Catholic
**Trigger:** the word **Apocrypha** as our default; “Rome added books at Trent”; deuterocanon in a penalty box; “you decide.”

**What they need:** their word — **deuterocanonical** — and their books in the Old Testament when their tradition pack is on. Trent named a dispute. It did not invent Tobit.

### E. Orthodox
**Trigger:** flattened to “Catholic plus extras”; ignoring that their list and their *use* of books is its own life.

**What they need:** their books named as read and holy in their liturgy, not as a curiosity drawer.

### F. Ethiopian Tewahedo
**Trigger:** Enoch as a museum piece; Meqabyan confused with Maccabees; “left out” language that implies their fathers were foolish.

**What they need:** an upper floor that is a home, not an exhibit.

### G. Pentecostal / charismatic
**Trigger:** dry museum tone; Spirit talked about only as a historical idea; Hard Ground that explains away healing or deliverance texts.

**What they need:** the Campfire to still burn. Room for the Spirit as teacher, not only the archaeologist.

### H. Black church and other preaching traditions
**Trigger:** academic English that talks down; Jesus reduced to ethics; lament and exodus treated as ancient sociology only.

**What they need:** the Campfire voice of deliverance, not only the passport.

### I. Messianic and Jewish-sensitive readers
**Trigger:** every Hebrew book immediately “about Jesus” before it is allowed to be itself; “Old Testament” said as if the first library is expired.

**What they need:** Thread to Jesus *last*, and the words **Hebrew Scriptures** available. Do not baptize Moses in the Door.

### J. Mainline / progressive
**Trigger:** Hard Ground that sanitizes violence and texts about women, slaves, or enemies; Jesus thread that only sounds like a 1950s tract; refusal to say “Christians have harmed with this verse.”

**What they need:** Hard Ground that is honest and not a takedown of the faith.

### K. Seekers and the wounded
**Trigger:** church-fight in the foyer; being told they must pick a chair before they have met Jesus.

**What they need:** Door, Campfire, World, Thread — then chairs if they ask.

## 3. Phrases we must retire or cage

These are already in our earlier notes. They will cost us sharing.

| Do not say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| make your own best judgment | see both chairs, then walk with your church and the Spirit |
| left out / taken out of the Bible | received in some churches, not received as Scripture in others |
| Apocrypha (as the house default) | deuterocanon / “books other churches read as Scripture” / “books many Protestants read for history” — use the user’s tradition word |
| the real ending / the fake verses | the shortest old copies end here; most later copies and most pulpits read on; here is why each side cares |
| scholars have shown | many careful readers argue… the church has long confessed… |
| myth, legend, fairy tale | origin story, campfire of beginnings, how this genre works |
| we cannot know what the Bible said | we have an embarrassment of copies; a handful of places the copies disagree; none of them erase Jesus |
| prove the Bible | the dirt can rhyme with the text; the text does not stand or fall on one shovel |
| WWJD as “be nice” | what this book shows Jesus doing with power, pain, enemies, and the poor |

“Does not prove” stays. It is good. Soften the *order*:
1. Here is the object.  
2. Here is how it helps you feel the book.  
3. Here is what it does not do.  
Never lead with the denial.

## 4. The Trust Card (first-run, one screen, cannot skip)

Title: **How this house treats the Bible**

Body, short enough to screenshot:

> We love this library. We receive it as the church’s Holy Scripture — God-breathed, able to make you wise for salvation.
>
> God used real people in real weather. That is not a threat to inspiration. That is how He chose to speak.
>
> Christians who love Jesus do not always agree on dates, authors, or which books belong on which shelf. We will not hide those family arguments. We will not mock either chair.
>
> This app is a teacher and a map. It is not your church, not your pastor, and not the Holy Spirit.
>
> If a room ever feels like someone is trying to take the Book from you, that room has failed. Tell us.

A link: *What we will never do* — sneer, pick a culture-war team, generate fake verses, call your grandmother naïve.

This card is how conservative sharers survive the first five minutes.

## 5. Tradition pack (the most important share-feature)

On first open, after the Trust Card:

**Which house did you grow up in?**
- Protestant / evangelical
- Catholic
- Orthodox
- Ethiopian Tewahedo
- I am just arriving
- I do not want to say

This changes **defaults**, not **truth**.

| Pack | Default shelf | Other books appear as |
|---|---|---|
| Protestant | 66 | “Read as Scripture in other churches” — never “fake” |
| Catholic | 73, deuterocanon in OT order | Protestant shorter list explained without insult |
| Orthodox | their received list | same courtesy |
| Tewahedo | 81, Enoch and Jubilees at home | other lists as smaller houses |
| Arriving | Mark first, 66 as the common hallway | wings labeled gently |

The user can walk any wing.
We do not surprise a Baptist kid with Enoch as if it were Genesis.
We do not surprise a Tewahedo user by hiding Enoch in a drawer labeled extra.

## 6. Room-by-room trust rules

**Door.** Reverent image from that book’s world. No sarcastic title. No “the Bible got this wrong.”

**Campfire.** Written as if it could be read aloud in a living room. If it would embarrass a 12-year-old’s faith, rewrite it.

**Passport.** Traditional column **on the left**. Academic range on the right. Never a single date in bold as if the debate is over.

**World.** Daily life serves the text. It does not swallow the text. “Assyria was weather” must not become “Israel invented God because Assyria was weather.”

**Genre.** “This is a song” is not “this did not happen.” Some songs remember real nights.

**Two Chairs.** Each chair must be one a pastor in that camp would recognize as their own, not a cartoon. End with: *the book still has work to do either way.* Never: *now you decide what is true.*

**Ink.** Open with abundance: thousands of Greek copies, early papyri, the blessing on silver. Then the handful of famous forks. Then: **no Christian doctrine hangs on a single disputed line.** That last sentence is how we keep KJV-only families in the room even when they disagree with our conclusion.

**Hard Ground.** Name the wound. Do not perform it. Path never gets gore. Do not use Hard Ground to prosecute the church. Do not use it to pretend the text is tame.

**Thread to Jesus.** Specific to the book. Include, over the whole library, the things BibleProject is accused of skipping when the book itself carries them: sin, holiness, the cross, a risen body, judgment, mercy. Do not make every Thread a systematic theology lecture. Do not make every Thread only “Jesus was kind.”

If Romans’ Thread never mentions that God justifies the ungodly, evangelicals will not share Romans.
If Exodus’ Thread never mentions a people set free, the Black church will not share Exodus.
Both can be true in one house.

## 7. Genesis is the share-killer if we are careless

Creation.com-style readers use Genesis 1–11 as a litmus test.
Progressive readers use it as a test of whether we are adults.

Do not try to win both with one sentence.

Do this:
- Campfire tells the story as Scripture tells it, with full weight.
- Genre room explains that Christians who submit to this book still differ on *how* the days and the flood sit next to the world God made.
- Two Chairs: a historical-young-earth chair and a literary-framework / old-earth chair, each held by people who pray.
- Neither chair is labeled “the grown-up view.”
- Thread: whatever chair you sit in, this book says the world is gift, humans bear a name, and the fracture is real.

If we sneer at either chair, half the church will not share us with their children.

## 8. Ink without sending people into a tailspin

The Bart Ehrman popular story is: “there are more variants than words.”
The pew hears: “we cannot trust a sentence.”

Our Ink room must tell the other half in the same breath:
- most variants are spelling
- a short list is famous (Mark 16, John 7:53–8:11, the Comma, a few verses)
- the body of Jesus, the empty tomb, the Shema, the cross are not sitting on those variants
- showing you the seam is how you learn the Book was copied by humans God refused to replace with a photocopier

Frame: **confidence through sunlight**, not confidence through hiding.

---

## 9. Canon wing without a conspiracy

Never:
- “they hid these books”
- “Constantine picked the Bible”
- “Rome added books to win a debate”

Always:
- communities received books they heard the Shepherd’s voice in
- lists grew and were argued
- Ethiopia kept a wider house
- Reformers narrowed the Old Testament to the Hebrew shelf
- Catholics named the deuterocanon in a crisis
- nobody in this story is a cartoon villain

A seeker who was told “the church banned Enoch” should leave the wing calmer, not more conspiratorial.
A Protestant who was told “Catholics added fake books” should leave ashamed of that sentence.

---

## 10. WWJD without the bracelet problem

Critics of WWJD say it shrinks Jesus into a mascot for nice decisions.

Safeguards:
- The companion answers from a book the user has walked, not from a mood.
- Thread rooms show Jesus using power, facing Satan, going to the cross, eating with the wrong people, refusing the booth’s kind of kingdom.
- Some answers will be “die to the thing you wanted,” not “follow your heart.”
- The companion may say “I will not play Jesus as your party’s mascot.”

People share a Jesus who is costly and alive.
They do not share a life-coach.

## 11. Why someone will actually press Share

People share Christian tools when four things are true at once:

1. **It made me love Him more**, not just know a fact.
2. **I would trust it with my kid.**
3. **It did not make me join a fight.**
4. **The first two minutes were beautiful.**

So the share unit is not “a thread about who left books out.”
The share unit is:

- the Capernaum morning
- the net-weight
- the short ending of Mark that still announces He is risen
- a Thread sentence you could pray
- “send this house to someone starting out”

Build a **Send a House** button on the Carry screen.
Pre-written, editable:

> I walked the Gospel of Mark in a thing called The House. It put me in a fishing town, showed me an old copy of the last page, and did not yell at me. If you have been thirsty, start here.

That is evangelism the grandmother understands.

---

## 12. What we will not sand down to keep peace

Honesty stays.
Two Chairs stay.
Dirt stays.
The Ethiopian floor stays.
“Does not prove” stays.
Hard Ground stays.

We change **order, words, defaults, and tone**.
We do not change the architecture into a tract or into a seminar that is embarrassed by Jesus.

If a user demands that we hide the short ending of Mark, we refuse.
If a user demands that we call Tobit a lie, we refuse.
If a user demands that we delete the cross from Romans, we refuse.

Hospitality is not the same as emptiness.

---

## 13. One-line test before any room ships

Read the Path layer out loud.
Ask:

- Would I send this to a new believer I love?
- Would a pastor who disagrees with my favorite chair still recognize their chair?
- Does this make Jesus larger, or only the controversy larger?

If the third answer is no, it does not ship — even if it is clever.
