# One object per book
## Excavated anchor + what it does not prove

Attach this block to every book module’s World room and object tray.

Format (always):
- **On the tray:** the find
- **Feel:** one sentence
- **Does not prove:** one sentence

Shared finds are allowed. The sentence must still be book-specific.

---

## Torah

### Genesis
**On the tray:** A Middle Bronze city gate and rampart (Shechem / Megiddo / Hazor class) — the kind of city-king world Abraham’s stories walk through.  
**Feel:** The ancestors are not camping in empty Sunday-school sand. They are resident aliens among walled towns that can feed or refuse them.  
**Does not prove:** Abraham, Sarah, or Joseph walked through that exact gate.

### Exodus
**On the tray:** Rekhmire’s brickyard painting (Thebes) plus a Ramesside quota text (“no straw,” daily brick counts).  
**Feel:** Oppression is mud, a mold, a number, and an overseer — not a cartoon chorus.  
**Does not prove:** These workers are the Hebrews of Moses, or which Pharaoh Exodus means.

### Leviticus
**On the tray:** A four-horned limestone altar from an Israelite site (e.g. Beersheba dismantled altar; Arad sanctuary).  
**Feel:** Holiness had corners you could put your hand on. Sacrifice was architecture, not a mood.  
**Does not prove:** This altar was authorized by Leviticus, or that Arad’s shrine was orthodox.

### Numbers
**On the tray:** Ketef Hinnom silver amulets (~600 BC) with the priestly blessing of Numbers 6.  
**Feel:** “The LORD bless you and keep you” was worn on a body before it was a wall plaque.  
**Does not prove:** Moses engraved these scrolls in the wilderness.

### Deuteronomy
**On the tray:** Nash Papyrus (Ten Words + Shema) and Qumran tefillin / mezuzot that bind Deuteronomy 6 to a body and a doorpost.  
**Feel:** “Hear, O Israel” was already a thing you strapped on and walked with.  
**Does not prove:** These copies are Moses’ first recitation on the plains of Moab.

## History

### Joshua
**On the tray:** A highland four-room house and collar-rim storage jar from early Iron Age villages.  
**Feel:** “Possessing the land” looks like cisterns, terraces, and a roof — not only falling walls.  
**Does not prove:** Joshua’s campaign calendar, or that a particular burn layer is his.

### Judges
**On the tray:** The same village house, often with few pig bones in highland sites.  
**Feel:** The book’s chaos happens in small clustered houses, not in caped anarchy.  
**Does not prove:** Absence of pig = “these people were already Israel” in every site (that inference is debated).

### Ruth
**On the tray:** A saddle quern / grindstone from an Iron Age floor (Tell Halif class).  
**Feel:** Gleaning and bread are a woman’s dawn and a poor family’s math.  
**Does not prove:** This stone sat in Naomi’s house in Bethlehem.

### 1 Samuel
**On the tray:** Shiloh’s storage and bone deposits from the late Bronze / Iron I shrine-town; plus later Khirbet Qeiyafa (Judahite border fortress, early Iron II — `debated` as “Davidic”).  
**Feel:** Holy place and outpost — a people between tribal shrine and king.  
**Does not prove:** The ark sat in the excavated rooms, or that Qeiyafa is David’s name on a map.

### 2 Samuel
**On the tray:** Tel Dan stele (9th c. BC) with the phrase “House of David.”  
**Feel:** David’s name is a dynasty other kings could curse in stone.  
**Does not prove:** The Large Stone Structure in Jerusalem is David’s palace.

### 1 Kings
**On the tray:** Samaria ivories (Omride luxury) and the Mesha Stele (Moab’s king naming Omri’s Israel and, in a debated line, possibly the house of David).  
**Feel:** Solomon’s glory in the text sits next to a later northern palace that actually left pretty bones of luxury — and a foreign king who brags he beat Israel.  
**Does not prove:** We have Solomon’s temple floor.

### 2 Kings
**On the tray:** Sennacherib’s Lachish reliefs (Nineveh palace) + Lachish Letter 4 (“we do not see Azekah”).  
**Feel:** Assyria and Babylon are a wall-carving of a city dying and an ostracon written while the next city dies.  
**Does not prove:** The relief depicts a biblical verse-for-verse, or that the letter is Jeremiah’s.

### 1 Chronicles
**On the tray:** A Yehud stamp impression (Persian-period jar handle from Judah).  
**Feel:** This book is a rebuilt people counting themselves after the burn — paper and jars, not a throne.  
**Does not prove:** Ezra wrote this handle.

### 2 Chronicles
**On the tray:** Hezekiah’s tunnel and the Siloam Inscription (two crews meeting in the rock).  
**Feel:** A king preparing water under siege — the Chronicler’s “trusted the LORD” has a wet tunnel.  
**Does not prove:** Every reform the Chronicler praises has a matching inscription.

### Ezra
**On the tray:** The Cyrus Cylinder (Babylon, Persian victory theology: peoples and gods sent home) + Yehud coins/seals.  
**Feel:** Return is imperial paperwork as well as a pilgrimage.  
**Does not prove:** The cylinder is the decree of Ezra 1 word-for-word (it is not a Jewish charter).

### Nehemiah
**On the tray:** Elephantine letter to Jerusalem’s leadership about a destroyed temple of YHW (410 BC).  
**Feel:** Persian-period Jews write petitions across empires. Walls and letters are the work.  
**Does not prove:** These papyri are Nehemiah’s correspondence about Jerusalem’s wall.

### Esther
**On the tray:** Persepolis Fortification tablets (rations, travel, royal women in the Persian machine) and the archaeology of Susa’s palace platform.  
**Feel:** A court that feeds and files people. Esther’s silence about God sits inside a very loud bureaucracy.  
**Does not prove:** A queen named Esther appears in the tablets.

## Poetry and wisdom

### Job
**On the tray:** Edomite / north-Arabian cult stands and inscriptions from the world the book names (Uz, Teman, Shuah as a map of east).  
**Feel:** The argument is set among eastern wise men, not in a synagogue classroom.  
**Does not prove:** A historical Job owned these objects.

### Psalms
**On the tray:** 11Q5 (Great Psalms Scroll) + Ketef Hinnom blessing.  
**Feel:** Israel’s prayers were copied, reordered, and worn. Psalm 151 lives in the dirt at Qumran.  
**Does not prove:** David held this scroll.

### Proverbs
**On the tray:** An inscribed Hebrew ostracon or seal from a Judahite house — names, deliveries, ordinary ink.  
**Feel:** Wisdom is for kitchens and gates, which is where writing shows up as lists.  
**Does not prove:** Solomon penned this sherd.

### Ecclesiastes
**On the tray:** 4QQohelet (2nd c. BC).  
**Feel:** “Under the sun” was already being copied while Greece was in the air.  
**Does not prove:** Qoheleth is Solomon, or that he is not.

### Song of Songs
**On the tray:** 4Q106–108 (Song fragments, some shorter than MT).  
**Feel:** Desire-poetry was Scripture enough to copy in the desert.  
**Does not prove:** The lovers are Solomon and a Shulammite we can name on a tomb.

## Major prophets

### Isaiah
**On the tray:** 1QIsaᵃ (Great Isaiah Scroll, ~125 BC) beside Sennacherib’s prism (Jerusalem shut up “like a bird in a cage,” 701 BC).  
**Feel:** The book survived as a whole scroll; the siege it remembers left an Assyrian caption.  
**Does not prove:** Isaiah of Jerusalem signed the Qumran copy, or that Sennacherib’s boast equals Isaiah’s ending.

### Jeremiah
**On the tray:** Lachish ostraca from the Babylonian destruction level.  
**Feel:** Fire-signals, a prophet in the air, a city about to go dark — Jeremiah’s decade in pottery ink.  
**Does not prove:** A “Baruch” bulla (some on the market are contested) is his secretary’s seal.

### Lamentations
**On the tray:** Jerusalem and Lachish 586 BC burn layers — ash, smashed jars, arrowheads.  
**Feel:** The poems have a color: black on stone.  
**Does not prove:** Jeremiah sat in that exact room writing chapter 1.

### Ezekiel
**On the tray:** An Āl-Yāhūdu barley/date contract with a Yahwistic name.  
**Feel:** Visions happen among people who still owe the canal.  
**Does not prove:** Ezekiel signed the tablet, or that Judah-town is the exact Chebar settlement.

### Daniel
**On the tray:** Jehoiachin ration tablet (court of Babylon) + early Daniel copies at Qumran (late 2nd / 1st c. BC).  
**Feel:** A Judean royal body was actually on a Babylonian meal list; the book was already being copied young.  
**Does not prove:** Which chair wins the 6th-vs-2nd-century debate.

## The Twelve

### Hosea
**On the tray:** Samaria ostraca (8th c. wine and oil shipments, Israelite names).  
**Feel:** The northern kingdom’s prosperity is jars moving through a palace economy — the comfort Hosea and Amos hate.  
**Does not prove:** Hosea loaded these jars.

### Joel
**On the tray:** A Judahite grain silo / threshing floor installation.  
**Feel:** Locusts are not a metaphor first. They are next year’s bread.  
**Does not prove:** The swarm of a given chapter.

### Amos
**On the tray:** Samaria ivories — luxury inlaid with other nations’ art.  
**Feel:** “Ivory houses” is not a flourish. The north left teeth of luxury in the dirt.  
**Does not prove:** Amos saw this panel.

### Obadiah
**On the tray:** An Edomite shrine or pottery assemblage (Horvat Qitmit / Busayra class).  
**Feel:** Edom is a people with their own holy things, not a cartoon cousin.  
**Does not prove:** The raid Obadiah names.

### Jonah
**On the tray:** Assyrian palace reliefs from Nineveh (Kuyunjik) — empire as carved terror and beauty.  
**Feel:** “Go to Nineveh” means walk toward the mouth of the machine.  
**Does not prove:** A prophet named Jonah preached in that throne room, or that the fish left a bone.

### Micah
**On the tray:** Lachish relief + Halif burn layer (701).  
**Feel:** Moresheth’s neighbor towns died in the campaign Micah’s century knew.  
**Does not prove:** Micah stood on that tell.

### Nahum
**On the tray:** Babylonian Chronicle tablet for the fall of Nineveh (612 BC).  
**Feel:** The taunt-song happened to a city that did fall.  
**Does not prove:** Nahum dated his poem to that week.

### Habakkuk
**On the tray:** 1QpHab — Habakkuk 1–2 copied and interpreted at Qumran.  
**Feel:** “How long?” was still being aimed at new empires generations later.  
**Does not prove:** Qumran’s enemy is Habakkuk’s Chaldeans.

### Zephaniah
**On the tray:** Ketef Hinnom amulets from Josiah’s wider generation.  
**Feel:** Jerusalem still wore YHWH’s name on silver while prophets said the day was coming.  
**Does not prove:** Zephaniah blessed these wearers.

### Haggai
**On the tray:** A Persian-period Yehud seal and the thin rebuilt-Jerusalem footprint of the late 6th century.  
**Feel:** “This house in ruins” matches a small, scrappy return, not a Hollywood temple.  
**Does not prove:** We have Zerubbabel’s foundation deposit.

### Zechariah
**On the tray:** Same Yehud seal / early second-temple stone work.  
**Feel:** Night visions land on a real, underwhelming building site.  
**Does not prove:** The menorah he saw is Magdala’s stone.

### Malachi
**On the tray:** Yehud coin (often with a lily or facing figure — Jewish province under Persia).  
**Feel:** “Where is the God of justice?” is asked in a province that can mint but cannot crown.  
**Does not prove:** The coin’s image is the “sun of righteousness.”

## Gospels and Acts

### Matthew
**On the tray:** Magdala Stone (menorah carved while the Temple still stood).  
**Feel:** Matthew’s “synagogue” and “fulfill” live in a Judaism that can already carve the Temple onto a block in Galilee.  
**Does not prove:** Jesus taught over this stone.

### Mark
**On the tray:** Lead net-weight + the Galilee working boat (type) + Capernaum basalt courtyard house.  
**Feel:** “Follow me” costs a night’s work. The village is stone and courtyard, not marble.  
**Does not prove:** This hull or this house was Peter’s.

### Luke
**On the tray:** Magdala’s first-century synagogue (coin of AD 43 under mosaic) and mikva’ot.  
**Feel:** Luke’s careful towns have baths and a meeting house a Gentile writer could still get right.  
**Does not prove:** The centurion of Luke 7 paid for this floor.

### John
**On the tray:** P52 (John 18 scrap) as text-object; Pilate inscription at Caesarea as power-object.  
**Feel:** The Word-made-flesh gospel is on papyrus within living memory of the grandfathers; the prefect who appears in John 18–19 is named in stone.  
**Does not prove:** P52 was copied in John’s house, or that the Pilate stone “confirms every sentence.”

### Acts
**On the tray:** Delphi **Gallio inscription** (proconsul of Achaia, dated) + Corinth’s **Erastus** pavement stone (“aedile”).  
**Feel:** Paul moves through named officers and civic donors, not a fog. Gallio in Acts 18 has a calendar hook.  
**Does not prove:** The Erastus of the pavement is the Erastus of Romans 16:23 (possible, not locked).

## Paul

### Romans
**On the tray:** A first-century Roman brick stamp / insula threshold — rented stacked housing of the capital.  
**Feel:** “All roads” is also a crowded block where Jew and Gentile share a stair.  
**Does not prove:** Phoebe knocked on this door.

### 1 Corinthians
**On the tray:** Remains of Corinth’s meat-market / temple neighborhood (shops against sacred precincts).  
**Feel:** “Food offered to idols” is a street smell, not a thought experiment.  
**Does not prove:** Paul bought dinner at this stall.

### 2 Corinthians
**On the tray:** A milestone or harbor installation on the Corinth–Cenchreae road.  
**Feel:** The letter travels with a body. Cenchreae is Phoebe’s town on a real water.  
**Does not prove:** The tear-stained draft passed this stone.

### Galatians
**On the tray:** An inscription from Roman Galatia / Pisidian Antioch class — emperor cult in the same towns Acts names.  
**Feel:** “Another gospel” is being offered in streets that already have a lord called Caesar.  
**Does not prove:** The South vs. North Galatia map.

### Ephesians
**On the tray:** Ephesus terrace houses and the Artemis precinct.  
**Feel:** “Principalities” have a marble address. Believers in workshops can see the temple from the slope.  
**Does not prove:** The letter was first read in a terrace dining room.

### Philippians
**On the tray:** Philippi’s via Egnatia paving and the colony’s Latin inscriptions.  
**Feel:** A Roman military colony is proud in Latin. “Citizenship in heaven” lands on a street that already boasts citizenship.  
**Does not prove:** Paul wrote chapter 2 in this block.

### Colossians
**On the tray:** Lycus Valley civic remains (Colossae itself is under-excavated; Laodicea and Hierapolis carry the region).  
**Feel:** The letter names a triangle of towns you can still walk.  
**Does not prove:** We have Philemon’s house plan at Colossae.

### 1 Thessalonians
**On the tray:** Thessalonian “politarch” inscriptions — the exact civic title Acts 17 uses.  
**Feel:** Luke’s city hall vocabulary matches the stones.  
**Does not prove:** Jason’s house is under that forum.

### 2 Thessalonians
**On the tray:** Same civic stones.  
**Feel:** “Stand firm” is written to a town with officials and rumors.  
**Does not prove:** Authorship.

### 1 Timothy
**On the tray:** An inscription honoring civic patrons / household benefactors from Ephesus or a similar Asian city.  
**Feel:** “Household of God” is being organized in a world that already knows households as public units.  
**Does not prove:** Paul ordained elders in this hall.

### 2 Timothy
**On the tray:** Mamertine-type prison tradition is late; better tray object: a Roman slave-collar or work-chain type + a first-century lamp.  
**Feel:** “Come before winter” is a real shipping calendar and a dark room.  
**Does not prove:** The Mamertine cell is Paul’s.

### Titus
**On the tray:** Gortyn / Cretan civic inscriptions (law code is earlier; Roman Crete still left a dense civic record).  
**Feel:** “Cretans” in the letter are island cities with old reputations, not a slur invented in a study.  
**Does not prove:** The quotation about Cretans is fair to every Cretan.

### Philemon
**On the tray:** A slave-sale tablet or collar *type* from the Roman East + a house-church sized peristyle.  
**Feel:** Onesimus is not a metaphor. People were property on paper.  
**Does not prove:** This tablet names him.

## General letters and Revelation

### Hebrews
**On the tray:** A Second Temple stone vessel and a mikveh step.  
**Feel:** “The way into the holy place” is being preached while stone cups and baths still teach purity with the hands.  
**Does not prove:** The date before/after 70.

### James
**On the tray:** A grindstone and a set of loom weights — the poor’s toolkit.  
**Feel:** “Your gold corrodes” is spoken over people who grind and weave.  
**Does not prove:** James the Just owned this courtyard.

### 1 Peter
**On the tray:** Yehohanan’s crucified heel (Jerusalem) as the empire’s method; a modest Asian house threshold for the recipients.  
**Feel:** “If you suffer as a Christian” has a nail in a bone behind it.  
**Does not prove:** Peter watched this execution.

### 2 Peter
**On the tray:** P72 (3rd/4th c.) as the oldest substantial copy.  
**Feel:** Even a disputed letter was copied early and bound with Peter and Jude.  
**Does not prove:** Simon Peter held the pen.

### 1 John
**On the tray:** P9 / early Johannine papyri + Ephesus-region house remains.  
**Feel:** “What we have looked at and touched” is a claim made in a city of touching marble gods.  
**Does not prove:** The Comma Johanneum (that is a late ink problem, not a dirt problem).

### 2 John / 3 John
**On the tray:** A traveler’s hospitality inscription / guest-house threshold type.  
**Feel:** These scraps are about who gets a bed.  
**Does not prove:** “The elect lady” is a named house we have.

### Jude
**On the tray:** Qumran Aramaic Enoch fragments (the booklet Jude quotes).  
**Feel:** Jude’s citation has a desert manuscript behind it, not a medieval rumor.  
**Does not prove:** Jude therefore canonizes all 108 chapters of 1 Enoch.

### Revelation
**On the tray:** Archaeology of the seven cities — especially Laodicea’s water system and Ephesus’ imperial cult architecture — plus Patmos as a real island.  
**Feel:** “Lukewarm” and “where Satan’s throne is” are local burns, not code for whatever we hate this week.  
**Does not prove:** A vision-map of New Jerusalem has a street address.

## Deuterocanon

### Tobit
**On the tray:** 4Q196–200 (Aramaic/Hebrew Tobit at Qumran).  
**Feel:** The story of a diaspora family was copied in the Judean desert in its older languages.  
**Does not prove:** Tobit of Nineveh is a census name.

### Judith
**On the tray:** A Hellenistic-period Judean / Samarian fortress type.  
**Feel:** A widow walking into a general’s camp is a tale told in an age that knew camps.  
**Does not prove:** The campaign’s geography is a war diary.

### Wisdom of Solomon
**On the tray:** Alexandrian-style Isis / Serapis household figurines vs. Jewish aniconic stone vessels — two religious streets in a Greek city.  
**Feel:** Wisdom is argued where images are for sale.  
**Does not prove:** The author taught in a specific gymnasium.

### Sirach
**On the tray:** Hebrew Ben Sira at Masada and Qumran.  
**Feel:** A Jerusalem teacher’s book existed in Hebrew before it was only a Greek church book.  
**Does not prove:** Jesus ben Sira’s classroom floor.

### Baruch / Letter of Jeremiah
**On the tray:** 7Q2 (Greek Letter of Jeremiah at Qumran).  
**Feel:** A warning about idols was already circulating in Greek among Judeans.  
**Does not prove:** Baruch son of Neriah wrote it in 586.

### 1 Maccabees
**On the tray:** Seleucid and Hasmonean coins; the architecture of a rededicated temple we infer more from text than from a “Hanukkah layer.”  
**Feel:** The revolt minted its own metal.  
**Does not prove:** We have Judas’s hammer.

### 2 Maccabees
**On the tray:** The same coins + Hellenistic torture / civic terror as known practice (no need for a snuff exhibit).  
**Feel:** Martyr stories grew in a real police state.  
**Does not prove:** Every miracle in the abridgment.

### Additions to Daniel / Esther
**On the tray:** Greek papyri of Esther; the shorter Hebrew Daniel at Qumran vs. longer Greek Daniel in the great codices.  
**Feel:** These extra scenes have a language and a library, not a Hebrew Qumran twin.  
**Does not prove:** Susanna’s courtroom is a Persian transcript.

### Psalm 151
**On the tray:** 11Q5, the Hebrew psalm itself.  
**Feel:** “A psalm of David when he was small” is not a late Greek doodle.  
**Does not prove:** David composed this exact poem behind the flock.

### Prayer of Manasseh
**On the tray:** Royal bullae and the archaeology of 7th-century Jerusalem (Manasseh’s long reign is historically plausible; the prayer is a later voice).  
**Feel:** A wicked king’s city was real. The prayer is how a later community put repentance in his mouth.  
**Does not prove:** We have Manasseh’s copy.

### 1 Esdras / 4 Ezra
**On the tray:** Same Persian Yehud kit / post-70 Jewish apocalyptic context.  
**Feel:** Paper empire and ruined temple are the two weathers these books breathe.  
**Does not prove:** Ezra the scribe authored 4 Ezra after 70 (the book uses his name).

## Tewahedo-distinct

### 1 Enoch
**On the tray:** 4Q201 and kin — Aramaic Watchers fragments.  
**Feel:** The angels-who-crossed-a-line booklet is Second Temple dirt, not an Ethiopian invention.  
**Does not prove:** Qumran owned the Parables / Son of Man booklet (it is missing there).

### Jubilees
**On the tray:** 4Q216–224 Hebrew Jubilees.  
**Feel:** A Sinai retelling with a 364-day clock was Scripture-adjacent for a desert sect.  
**Does not prove:** Moses received this dictation on the mountain.

### 1–3 Meqabyan
**On the tray:** There is no DSS object. Use a Ge’ez manuscript photograph as a *transmission* object, not an excavation.  
**Feel:** These books lived in Ethiopian copying rooms.  
**Does not prove:** They are the Greek Maccabees under another name. They are not.

### 4 Baruch
**On the tray:** 586 and 70 destruction layers in Jerusalem (the book’s wound, twice).  
**Feel:** A sequel to Jeremiah keeps being written when the city falls again.  
**Does not prove:** Baruch hid the temple vessels in the way the tale says.
