# Oldest surviving witness, book by book

Dates are paleographic ranges, not signatures.
“Oldest” means the oldest *copy of this book* (or of a clear quotation), not the date the book was first written.

DSS copy-counts are rounded from published tallies; specialists differ by a few.

Legend  
**DSS** Dead Sea / Judean Desert  
**LXX** Greek Old Testament  
**MT** medieval Hebrew (Leningrad 1008 unless noted)  
**NT pap** Greek New Testament papyrus

## Torah

| Book | Oldest wording / copy | What it holds | Other early witnesses | Compare-note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis | DSS from ~250–150 BC (e.g. 6Q1, 4QGen) | Fragments; ~20–24 copies at Qumran | P.Fouad 266a (Greek Gen, 1st c. BC); later MT | Creation wording is stable in the opening; details of names and ages sometimes shift with LXX |
| Exodus | DSS mid-3rd c. BC (4Q17 Exod–Lev among the earliest) | ~17–21 copies; some paleo-Hebrew | 7Q1 Greek Exod; Nash mixes Decalogue | Sea of Reeds / route names are geography, not a single labeled papyrus |

## The Twelve
A Hebrew Twelve scroll tradition + **8ḤevXIIgr** (Greek, ~50 BC–AD 50).  
DSS copies of the Twelve as a set: ~8–10.

Oldest fragments reach Hosea, Amos, Micah, Jonah, Nahum, Habakkuk (plus the famous Habakkuk *pesher* commentary), Zephaniah, Zechariah, Malachi, etc., mostly 2nd–1st c. BC.

Habakkuk 1–2 at Qumran is both biblical text and sectarian commentary — teach the difference.

## How to show this in a book module

Add a room-strip called **The Oldest Copy** under The Ink:

```
Oldest copy we can touch: [name] ([date range])
Language: 
How much survives: fragment / many chapters / nearly all
Agrees mainly with: MT / LXX / its own way / too little to say
One place it differs (if any):
What we still do not have: the author’s first sheet
```

If the book is Esther: say plainly “no Dead Sea copy yet found.”
If the book is 1 Enoch: show Aramaic fragment + Ge’ez complete tradition as two columns.
If the book is John: show P52 photo-credit and the verses it actually contains — nothing more.
