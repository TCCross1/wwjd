# Engulf
## How the House becomes a place people do not want to leave

A user becomes engulfed when they forget they opened an app and start to feel they entered a house.
Enjoyment here is not entertainment. It is *I was taken care of, I saw something, I want to go back.*

Streaks, XP, and verse-loot will get a week of taps and a quiet uninstall.
Place, memory, beauty, and a companion who does not rush them will get a life.

---

## The feeling we are aiming at

Someone sits down for twelve minutes.
They look up and it has been forty.
They can still tell you the grindstone, the two chairs, and one sentence about Jesus.
They open the app the next night without a reminder, because the house is unfinished in a good way — like a novel, not like a chore chart.

---

## 1. Arrive in weather, not in a menu

Cold open.
No hamburger of 66 books.

Tonight the threshold is a courtyard at the hour it actually is on their phone.
If it is 6 a.m. in Kentucky, Mark’s grindstone is already going.
If it is night, lamps. If it is Friday evening, a Sabbath hush on Jewish and Messianic packs.

One line only:
**The house is open. Which door?**

Three tiles, large stills, no paragraphs:
- Finish the house you already started
- I have something on my chest
- Walk a new book

The library is a side door. It is never the greeting.

---

## 2. The companion is a host, not a chatbot bubble

Name it in the house. The WWJD layer already exists. Give it a porch.

It does three host things:
- Remembers the object they carried last time (“you left with the net-weight”)
- Will not quote a book they have not entered
- Knows when to be quiet and put a still on the table instead of a paragraph

Voice: warm, unhurried, no youth-group hype, no lecture.
After a hard room it asks one question, not five.
After Thread it does not altar-call. It says: *take one thing. the door will be here tomorrow.*

People stay where they feel hosted.

---

## 3. Path is a picture-story you tap with your thumb

Visual learners live here.

Campfire is not text with a picture.
Campfire is five stills. Each tap is a breath. Caption is one spoken sentence, also printed.

World is a horizontal dawn-to-dark they drag with a finger.
The object lifts off the tray when they hold it — a small haptic, a one-line feel, then “does not prove.”

A person who never reads a long screen still walked Mark.

Audio sits under every Path visit: the Campfire read aloud in a human voice, slow, with room tone of that era (millstone, harbor, canal). Headphones make the house bigger than the phone.

---

## 4. Rooms you want to linger in

Linger is designed.

- **Map** pans like a tablecloth, not like Google. Then/now dissolve.
- **Ink** lets them slide a finger across two copies of the same line and watch one word change color.
- **Two Chairs** are actually chairs. Sit in one. Hear it at full strength. Sit in the other. The table does not declare a winner. People stay because they were trusted.
- **Hard Ground** is a closed door they may open. Opening is a choice. That choice is respect, and respect is sticky.

Never lock a room behind a quiz.
Curiosity is the only key.

---

## 5. Carry is a shelf, not a trophy case

They leave with a sentence, an object, or a question.
Those sit on a wooden shelf in the app — ten things, not a thousand badges.

Tap last month’s net-weight and you are back in Capernaum in one breath.
That is rereading without being told to reread.

Share the shelf item as a still + one line, not a screenshot of a fight.

The shelf is how the House becomes *theirs.*
Pokemon-of-verses is how it becomes trash.

---

## 6. Life questions as weather, not search

“I have something on my chest” opens a short honest list:

grief · anger · money · a child · a marriage · fear of dying · a church that hurt me · I do not believe · I want to start

Each one walks them to a Door, not a verse grenade.
Revenge → Jonah’s harbor.
Grief → Lamentations’ ash, or John 11’s tomb mouth.
Fear of the state → Daniel’s window, or Mark’s booth.

After the visit the companion may speak.
Not before.

This is how a thirsty person gets lost in the house without knowing the names of the books.

---

## 7. Return visits that change

Second time in Mark, the Door is the same courtyard at a different hour.
A side door that was locked (Ink, Hard Ground) now has a thin line of light.
The companion says only: *you have stood in the street. the chairs are ready if you want them.*

Seasonal weather:
- Advent stills go lantern-warm on the porch
- Holy Week darkens the Jerusalem maps
- Pentecost loosens Acts’ fabric
- Tisha B’Av and Lent both know ash — labeled with care, never flattened into each other

The house is alive. Dead apps feel the same every Tuesday.

---

## 8. Two people, one house

Family mode we already locked: Path on the left, Trail on the right.
Add presence, not a social network.

A parent sends a house. The kid walks Path. A small lamp appears on the parent’s shelf: *they stood in the courtyard.*
No feed. No comments. No pastor-surveillance dashboard.

A couple can walk Jonah on two phones and meet at Two Chairs.
That is church without a login wall.

---

## 9. The Watch door as a fire, then back to our rooms

BibleProject (or the pack’s neighbor film) plays in a frame on the wall, then the House asks one thing:

*What did their pictures leave out that our street showed you?*

One box. Optional. This turns a borrowed cartoon into our conversation and keeps the user inside.

Never dump them onto YouTube and lose them to the next recommended video. Return button is large: **Back into this house.**

---

## 10. Tiny daily bread, huge weekly table

Daily: 90 seconds. One still. One caption. One carried line from a house they have already entered. No guilt if they miss a day. Missed days do not break a chain because there is no chain.

Weekly: a full twelve-minute visit. The app may whisper on Sunday afternoon, once: *the next door is ready when you are.*

People enjoy what does not punish them.

---

## 11. Beauty that is specific, not stock

The stills we already specified are the hook.
A grindstone in basalt light will hold a thumb longer than a verse in Helvetica.

Object tray pieces should feel like you could pick them up.
Maps should feel drawn on a table.
Ink fragments should feel like glass over a museum case.

If it looks like a template from an app store “religion” category, we failed the engulf test.

---

## 12. Let them hear the words

A “read it to me” control on every Path room.
Voice: older than a YouTuber, younger than a lecture. Room tone under it.

For Psalms: they hear the psalm.
For Mark: they hear the Campfire, then a short public-domain stretch of the chapter if we have license.

Visual people often *are* audio people. They are not text people. We have been writing as if type is the meal.

---

## 13. A last page that opens outward

Carry screen always offers three quiet nexts, never a “keep your streak”:

- Stand in the street of this book one more minute (World replay)
- Sit in the chairs
- Ask the host what Jesus does with what you just saw

The third one is the companion, earned.
That is how WWJD and the teaching house become one enjoyment instead of two products stapled together.

---

## What we will not add to get them “hooked”

- Streak flames
- Leaderboards
- Unlock-the-next-book-by-quiz
- Push notifications that shame
- A verse-of-the-day that ignores which house they are in
- AI that invents a midrash and calls it Scripture
- A social feed of hot takes

Those engulf for a weekend.
They do not make a person love the text.

---

## The only test

Hand the phone to someone who does not like to read.
If they can walk Mark on pictures, sound, and a thumb — and they look up asking for Jonah — the House works.

If they ask where the points are, we built the wrong thing.
