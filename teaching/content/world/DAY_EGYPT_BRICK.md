# Sample day — brick in Egypt
**Era:** `empire_egypt`  
**Book overlay:** Exodus 1–5  
**Seats:** a Hebrew woman in the labor gang’s house; an overseer; a midwife on call  
**Badges:** `from the text` + `from neighbor-text` + `careful guess` on the exact hour-times

Use this as the model for every Dawn-to-dark card.

---

## Path (one screen)

It is still dark. The Nile air is already heavy.
A woman sets leftover bread beside a jar. The men will eat while walking.
Today’s number is bricks. Yesterday the quota did not drop. Straw is now their problem too.
A baby slept. She does not say out loud what Pharaoh said about sons.
Night will smell like river and smoke. Morning will smell like wet clay.

**This year’s fear:** the river that feeds Egypt is being used against our boys.

---

## Trail — dawn to dark

**Before light.** Fuel for a small fire — dung or reed. Bread from yesterday if any remains. Beer-like grain drink for calories, not a party.

**Walk.** Labor is not “at the office.” It is a place you walk to while it is still cool.

**The pit.** Mud, water, straw. Hands and a wooden mold. An overseer counts. The sun becomes an enemy around mid-morning. Work breaks when heat is stupid, then returns.

**The other room, same hour.** In a painted court someone is being told he is the son of a god and the Nile rises for him. He will never make a brick. Exodus wants you to feel both rooms.

**Women’s day is not off.** Water from the river. Grain to grind if they have it. A midwife may be called at any hour. Exodus names Shiphrah and Puah because the empire’s policy runs through a birth stool.

**Evening.** Shoulders done. A shared dish if there is one. Stories. The old name of Joseph is either comfort or a taunt — “a Pharaoh arose who did not know.”

**Night.** Heat leaves slowly. Someone wonders whether a baby should even be given a name yet.

## Pressure map

```
Pharaoh (god-king, owner of the narrative)
  → taskmasters / store-city officials
    → Hebrew foremen (the squeeze is passed down)
      → this household
```

What empire sells as a gift: order, grain, monuments that will last.
What it extracts: backs, babies, memory.
What this house fears: becoming only a quota.

## What not to do

Do not costume this as a cartoon slave chorus.
Do not name a Pharaoh if the book will not.
Do not skip the Egyptian poor who also baked in that sun — the book’s fight is with the palace’s claim, not with every person on the Nile.
Do not turn the ten plagues into a special-effects reel in this room. This room is the *ordinary* oppression the plagues answer.
