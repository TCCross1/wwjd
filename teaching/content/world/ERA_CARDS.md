# Era cards
Reusable world packs. Books borrow one or two. They do not rewrite Egypt every time.

Each card is Trail length. Path uses only Ground, Table, Fear. Summit may open the neighbor-text line.

---

## Ancestor road
**Feel:** tents, wells, honor, a household moving through other people’s cities.  
**Ground:** not empty desert. Named towns (Shechem, Hebron, Beersheba), grazing rights, a cave bought for burial. Water is a treaty.  
**Table:** bread, milk, a calf when guests come. Meat is hospitality or vow, not routine.  
**House:** a mobile *beit av*. Servants and extra wives are part of the economic unit, which is why Hagar’s story hurts inside the house, not outside it.  
**Power:** local kings, Egypt as the famine magnet, household gods you can steal (Rachel). No Torah-state yet.  
**Fear this year:** no son, no well, no grave in the land that was promised.  
**Desk:** these stories were told long before they were a book. The “author” is a people remembering a road.  
**Neighbor-text:** other ancient Near Eastern family and treaty customs; Nuzi-type household tablets are analogies, not photocopies — badge `careful guess`.

## Empire Egypt
**Feel:** the river is life and the palace owns the story of the river.  
**Ground:** mud-brick, reed, dust, the green ribbon of the Nile against desert. Store-cities. Heat that splits the day in two.  
**Table:** bread and beer as wages in kind. Onion, fish for some. Palace tables look like another planet.  
**House:** slave quarters and overseer math. Midwives work at night. A basket in reeds is a last idea, not a nursery theme.  
**Power:** Pharaoh as son of the gods. Brick quota is policy you feel in your shoulders.  
**Fear this year:** sons thrown in the river; quota raised; you will die in the same mud you were born in.  
**Desk:** Israel will tell this as a people who got out. Egyptians told their own story in temple reliefs where the king never loses. Two rooms.  
**Neighbor-text:** New Kingdom brick-making scenes; Semitic workers in Egyptian records. Exact Pharaoh of the exodus is disputed — do not fake a nameplate.

## Hill village (Iron Age Israel / Judah)
**Feel:** a small ring of houses, goats in the ground floor, bread every dawn.  
**Ground:** four-room / pillared house. Courtyard work. Cistern. Terraces on a limestone hill. Village maybe a few hundred people.  
**Table:** barley or wheat bread (the word for bread is almost the word for food). Oil, wine, lentils, milk and cheese from goat and fat-tail sheep. Meat on feast days.  
**Clock:** rain is theology. If the early rain fails, next year’s argument with God has already started.  
**Power:** before kings — elders in the gate. After kings — a cut of grain and sons for wars. Later — Assyria as weather from the north.  
**Fear this year:** raid, drought, a child dead of a fever you cannot name, a landlord in a year when the monarchy is strong.  
**Desk:** most villagers will hear a prophet; they will not read him. Writing lives with priests, court, and a few.  
**From the ground:** grindstones, loom weights, collar-rim jars, the house plan itself.

## Two kingdoms
**Feel:** the same houses as the village card, plus a gate where prophets shout and palaces that smell different.  
**Power weather:** Egypt fading as boss, then **Assyria** (deportations, speed, terror), then **Babylon**.  
**Two rooms:** Samaria’s ivory and a hill farmer who will never see it; Jerusalem’s temple and a widow’s oil.  
**Fear this year:** “they took the next town.” Amos and Micah are not abstract ethics. They are sermons in a market that still has poor at the edge.  
**Desk:** royal annals exist in every empire. Israel’s twist is that the court history *judges the court*.

## Exile Babylon
**Feel:** you can eat and still be ruined.  
**Ground:** flat alluvium, canals, barley and date palmgroves. Villages of deportees, including one the tablets call Āl-Yāhūdu — “Judah-town.”  
**Table:** barley, dates, beer. Plow animals are treasure. Meat uncommon.  
**Power:** land-for-service. You farm a plot; you owe tax, labor, sometimes a body for the army. Credit sharks exist because the tax day does not care that the canal was low.  
**Two rooms:** Jehoiachin on a palace ration list in the capital; a farmer named with a Yahwistic name on a clay contract near Nippur.  
**Fear this year:** the temple is gone and our grandchildren may speak only Akkadian.  
**Desk:** Ezekiel sees visions among the exiles. Psalm 137 is a song that refuses cheap cheer. Jeremiah’s letter says plant gardens anyway.  
**Neighbor-text:** Āl-Yāhūdu cuneiform archive (6th–5th c. BC); Babylonian ration tablets naming “Ya’u-kīnu” and sons.

## Persian return
**Feel:** a small province, a far king, a lot of documents.  
**Power:** Persia sells itself as restoration. The price is loyalty and tribute. Governors, papers, horses.  
**Fear this year:** the wall is a joke and the neighbors write letters against us. Identity has to be rebuilt with a book and a gate.  
**Desk:** Ezra is a scribe. This era makes Scripture-as-public-reading normal.  
**Esther’s weather:** a different Persian room — palace whim, ethnic danger, God’s name unprinted.

## Greek pressure
**Feel:** beautiful coercion.  
**Power:** language, sports, “reasonable” religion, then (under Antiochus IV) an altar that dares the holy place.  
**Fear this year:** my son wants to look Greek; my priest is being asked to choose.  
**Desk:** Daniel’s visions, Sirach’s classroom, Jubilees’ calendar, Enoch’s Watchers — all different answers to the same weather.  
**Neighbor-text:** 1 Maccabees as history-voice; 2 Maccabees as theology-voice.

## Herod and Rome (Gospels)
**Feel:** small villages under a polished boot.  
**Ground:** Galilee — agriculture first, fishing as hard work with a cut taken at the harbor. Houses of stacked fieldstone, shared courts, almost no mosaic floors in places like Capernaum. Alleys dusty or muddy. Population of a village: hundreds to a couple thousand.  
**Table:** bread dipped in stew, olives, figs, fish when the catch and the tax allow, local wine. Grinding time is a woman’s dawn.  
**Clock:** Sabbath is a real stop. Pilgrimage feasts empty the north toward Jerusalem.  
**Power chain:** Tiberius (face on some coins) → procurator in Judea / Antipas in Galilee → city elites and temple leadership → toll booth. Crucifixion is public speech: this is what happens to threats.  
**Fear this year:** debt, a sick child, a soldier’s right to make you carry a pack, the next feast in a tense city.  
**Desk:** good news is first a spoken thing in markets and houses. A gospel is a performed announcement, later a book.  
**From the ground:** basalt millstones, lead net-weights, first-century houses under later limestone, coins.

## Synagogue and the Greek street (letters + Revelation)
**Feel:** Jews and new believers in cities that already have a story about peace.  
**Ground:** workshops with rooms behind; rented halls; a riverbank when there is no synagogue building; islands like Patmos.  
**Power:** Caesar’s peace, guild feasts with a god at the table, slander that Christians hate mankind because they will not do the pinch of incense.  
**Fear this year:** lose the guild, lose the name, lose the body.  
**Desk:** Paul dictates; Tertius writes; Phoebe walks. Revelation is meant to be *heard* in a meeting, not decoded alone on a phone — irony the app should admit and then earn.
