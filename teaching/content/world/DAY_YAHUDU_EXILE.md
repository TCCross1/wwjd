# Sample day — Judah-town on a Babylonian canal
**Era:** `exile_babylon`  
**Book overlay:** Ezekiel, Psalm 137, Jeremiah 29  
**Seats:** a small farmer with a Yahwistic name on a clay contract; a woman in the same house; a king on a ration list far away in the capital  
**Badges:** `from neighbor-text` (Āl-Yāhūdu tablets, palace ration lists) + `from the text`

This day kills two lies: “exile was only chains” and “exile was fine, actually.”

---

## Path

You live in a village people call Judah-town, on a canal that is not the Jordan.
You grow barley and dates. You owe a cut. You can marry, plant, fight with a neighbor about a plow animal.
Jerusalem is a memory you can taste and cannot walk to.
Someone still says the old blessing over bread. Someone else has started giving their child a Babylonian nickname for market days.

**This year’s fear:** our grandchildren will not know how to sing the songs.

---

## Trail — dawn to dark

**Light on water.** Work starts early because the plain will steam. Irrigation is life. A neglected sluice is a theological problem wearing muddy clothes.

**The plot.** Land-for-service: the empire parked you here on purpose. You farm; you pay; you may owe labor or a substitute body for the king’s work. If the yield is thin you borrow dates or barley from a man who smiles like a cousin and collects like a palace.

**Noon.** Shade if you have a date palm. Bread, onion, thick beer. Meat is a story you tell about festivals at home.

**The other room, same empire.** In Babylon the city, “Ya’u-kīnu, king of Judah” appears on a ration tablet with his sons. He eats from the palace. He is alive. He is not free. Two-room illustration for 2 Kings 25:27–30.

**Evening.** A contract sealed in clay. Names that still have *Yahu* in them sit next to Babylonian gods’ names in the same archive. Identity is being negotiated in handwriting.

**Night.** Psalm 137 is not sung every night. Some nights you sleep. Some nights you cannot. Jeremiah’s letter is the scandal: build houses. Plant gardens. The city that burned is not the end of obedience.

---

## Pressure map

```
King of Babylon
  → provincial officials / canal administration
    → tax and credit men
      → the holder of the plot
        → hired help / family
```

What empire sells as a gift: land, a future, even rations for a captured king.
What it extracts: produce, service, the next generation’s accent.
What this house fears: forgetting Zion *and* starving if they only remember Zion.

## Why this changes how you hear the books

Ezekiel is not floating in “spiritual exile.” He is a priest without a house, among people who have plots and weddings.
Lamentations is for the city that was opened like a sack.
Isaiah 40 is for the children of this village, not for a poster.
Esther will be a later Persian chapter of the same question: how do you stay a people far from the land.
